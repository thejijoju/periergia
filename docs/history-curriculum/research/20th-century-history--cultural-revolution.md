## 1. Background and Mao's motives (keep the Leap brief)
- Seven Thousand Cadres Conference, January 1962 (over 7,000 participants): Mao made a self-criticism ("the person primarily responsible should be me") but insisted class struggle persisted throughout the socialist stage; afterwards Mao "took a backseat in economic matters" while Liu Shaoqi led recovery. [source: CR]
- Liu, July 1962, to Mao (as reported in CR): "History will record the role you and I played in the starvation of so many people, and the cannibalism will also be memorialized!" — treat as reported speech, not a verified quotation. [source: CR]
- International context: Sino-Soviet split; Mao regarded Khrushchev as a "revisionist" after the 1956 denunciation of Stalin; CCP published nine polemics against the USSR from 1963. [source: CR]
- Prelude: Wu Han's play *Hai Rui Dismissed from Office* (1959–61) was read as an allegory of Peng Dehuai's dismissal after the Lushan Conference. Yao Wenyuan's attack on it was published in Shanghai's *Wenhui Bao* on 10 November 1965 (commissioned secretly by Mao via Jiang Qing; CR says Feb 1965 commission, Zhang Chunqiao also involved per MN). Peng Zhen's "Five Man Group" issued the "February Outline" (12 February 1966) to keep the debate "academic"; Mao denounced it. [source: CR; MN; GOF]
- Interpretation of motives: MacFarquhar & Schoenhals see the movement as neither purely ideological nor merely a power struggle, citing the Sino-Soviet split, Khrushchev's ouster and the Great Leap catastrophe, and conclude it was at least partly a legacy project to cement Mao's place in history. Rebecca Karl: for Mao, power was a means of making revolution. [source: EVAL]

## 2. Launch, May–August 1966
- **16 May Notification** (Circular of 16 May): issued at an expanded Politburo session; drafted by Chen Boda with major revisions by Mao (who added the "sleeping beside us" paragraph); dissolved the Five Man Group, condemned Peng Zhen and created the Cultural Revolution Group (CRG). Secret at first; published in *People's Daily* on 17 May 1967. Usually treated as the start of the Cultural Revolution. [source: MN; CR]
- Key passage: "Those representatives of the bourgeoisie who have sneaked into the Party, the government, the army, and various spheres of culture are a bunch of counter-revolutionary revisionists... persons like Khrushchev for example, who are still nestling beside us." [source: CR (MacFarquhar & Schoenhals p. 47)]
- Purge of the "Peng–Luo–Lu–Yang Anti-Party Clique" (Peng Zhen, Luo Ruiqing, Lu Dingyi, Yang Shangkun). [source: CR]
- 1 June 1966 *People's Daily* editorial "Sweep Away All Cow Demons and Snake Spirits" (by Chen Boda) — first use of "Four Olds". [source: FOUR; CR]
- **Nie Yuanzi's poster**: 25 May 1966, Nie (philosophy department party secretary at Peking University, b. 1921) was lead signatory of a big-character poster attacking university president Lu Ping, Song Shuo and Peng Peiyun; it was encouraged by Kang Sheng's wife Cao Yi'ou. Mao ordered it broadcast nationally and published in *People's Daily* (about a week later) and called it "the first Marxist[-Leninist] big-character poster" in China. Nie was later jailed (1978), sentenced in 1983 to 17 years, paroled 1986, died 28 August 2019 aged 98. [source: NIE; CR]
- First "Red Guards": students at Tsinghua University High (Middle) School, signing posters 25 May–2 June 1966 (Zhang Chengzhi among leaders). Mao wrote them a letter of support on 1 August 1966. [source: RG]
- Classes suspended in Beijing, then nationwide (decision of 13 June). Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping sent "work teams" to schools; Mao, after swimming the Yangtze at Wuhan (July), returned and ordered the teams withdrawn (24 July). [source: CR]
- **"Bombard the Headquarters – My Big-Character Poster"**: written by Mao on 5 August 1966 during the 11th Plenum of the 8th Central Committee (opened 1 August); published in *People's Daily* only a year later (5 August 1967). Universally understood to target Liu Shaoqi (and Deng). At the plenum Lin Biao became No. 2; Liu fell from second to eighth. [source: BOMB; CR]
- **Sixteen Points** ("Decision Concerning the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution"), adopted 8 August 1966: a revolution "that touches people to their very souls"; aim to "overthrow those persons in authority who are taking the capitalist road". [source: CR]
- Slogan: "to rebel is justified" (造反有理). [source: CR]
- **Rallies**: 18 August 1966, over a million Red Guards in and around Tiananmen Square; Mao wore army uniform and a Red Guard armband pinned by Song Binbin; Lin Biao gave the keynote (endorsing destruction of the Four Olds). First of eight rallies, August–November 1966, drawing about 12 million people in total (CR); RG gives Red Guard strength as 11–12 million. The "Great Exchange of Revolutionary Experience" gave Red Guards free travel, food and lodging (Sept 1966–early 1967). [source: CR; RG; FOUR]
- Song Binbin anecdote: Mao asked about her name ("Binbin" = refined/gentle) and suggested "Yaowu" ("be martial"); *Guangming Daily* (20 Aug 1966) ran "I Put a Red Armband on Chairman Mao" under the name Song Yaowu. Song later said the name was imposed on her. [source: SGB; AUG]
- *Quotations from Chairman Mao* (Little Red Book): compiled by the PLA's General Political Department, internal edition May 1964 with Lin Biao's inscription; per CR, 350 million copies printed by December 1967. [source: CR; LRB = Quotations_from_Chairman_Mao_Tse-tung]

## 3. Red August, teachers and intellectuals
- 22 August 1966: Mao approved a Ministry of Public Security document forbidding police intervention against student movements; Minister Xie Fuzhi (26 Aug) said it was not wrong for Red Guards to beat "bad people". [source: AUG; CR]
- **Red August death toll (Beijing)**: official figures published 1980 — 1,772 killed (Aug–Sept 1966), 33,695 homes ransacked, 85,196 families driven out of Beijing; official figures published November 1985 — 10,275 dead, 92,000 homes ransacked, 125,000 families expelled. Daxing massacre, 27 Aug–1 Sept 1966: 325 killed, aged 80 years to 38 days; 22 families wiped out. [source: AUG; CR] — **sources differ; give both figures.**
- **Bian Zhongyun** (b. 19 June 1916), first vice-principal / deputy principal of the Girls' Middle School attached to Beijing Normal University, beaten to death by her female students on 5 August 1966 — the first educator killed by Red Guards in Beijing. Song Binbin (daughter of senior leader Song Renqiong) led the school's Red Guards but denied taking part in the killing. Bian's husband Wang Jingyao kept her blood-stained clothes and pursued the case for decades (the clothes detail is NOT verified here — omit). Song apologised publicly on 12 January 2014 at the school; Wang Jingyao rejected it as hypocritical. Song died in New York, 16 September 2024. [source: BIAN; SGB]
- Bian's killing: struggle session, beaten with nail-studded clubs, left in a rubbish cart; she died that day. [source: BIAN]
- **Lao She** (Shu Qingchun, 1899–1966), Manchu author of *Rickshaw Boy* (1936) and *Teahouse* (1957): paraded and beaten by Red Guards at the Temple of Confucius in Beijing; found drowned in Taiping Lake on 24 August 1966. Official record: suicide; Leo Ou-fan Lee raised the possibility of murder — circumstances unverified. Posthumously rehabilitated 1978. [source: LAO]
- Others who died in Red August–autumn 1966: translator Fu Lei and his wife (suicide, 3 September 1966, Shanghai); Zhou Zuoren and Chen Mengjia also listed among persecuted writers who killed themselves or attempted to. [source: AUG]
- Intellectuals labelled the "Stinking Old Ninth"; Gang of Four prosecution: 142,000 cadres and teachers in education persecuted; 229 members of the Chinese Academy of Sciences died; more than 2,600 people in arts and literature persecuted by the Ministry of Culture; 200 writers and artists persecuted to death were commemorated in 1979. [source: CR]
- Five Black Categories: landlords, rich peasants, counter-revolutionaries, "bad elements", rightists. [source: FP]
- Struggle sessions: public denunciation rallies, often by colleagues, students, spouses, children; sometimes fatal. Researchers estimate 100,000–200,000 suicides in the early Cultural Revolution. [source: STR; CR]
- Schools closed 1966; universities closed until 1970, most until 1972; national entrance exams (gaokao) abolished; admission of "worker-peasant-soldier students" by recommendation; gaokao restored in 1977. [source: CR; WYQ]

## 4. The Four Olds and heritage
- Four Olds: old ideas, old culture, old customs, old habits; term first used in the 1 June 1966 *People's Daily* editorial; endorsed by Lin Biao at the 18 August rally. Street and personal renaming; Chinese New Year celebrations banned. [source: FOUR]
- Beijing: of 6,843 cultural sites protected by a 1958 Beijing decision, 4,922 were damaged or destroyed; of 80 municipal-protected sites, 30 destroyed. AUG adds 2.3 million books burned and 3.3 million paintings, art objects and pieces of furniture destroyed in Beijing. [source: CR; AUG]
- The Party never formally sanctioned destruction; Central Committee issued protective "suggestions" on 14 May 1967. [source: CR]
- **Qufu**: in November 1966 Red Guards from Beijing Normal University led by Tan Houlan attacked the Temple and Cemetery of Confucius; the corpse of the 76th-generation Duke Yansheng, Kong Lingyi (1872–1919), was dug up and hung naked from a tree; many temple sculptures destroyed in 1966. Site is now UNESCO World Heritage (1994). [source: QUFU; CQ]
- Religion: temples, churches, mosques, monasteries closed, looted or converted; public Christian worship banned in 1966. [source: CR; LAO (Three-Self paragraph)]

## 5. Fall of Liu Shaoqi, Deng Xiaoping and other leaders
- October 1966 central work conference: Liu and Deng identified with a "bourgeois reactionary line", made self-criticisms. December 1966 Jiang Qing called Liu "China's Khrushchev". [source: LIU]
- 3 January 1967 first struggle session against Liu and wife Wang Guangmei in Zhongnanhai; Liu held up the state constitution, protesting his rights as head of state. Last meeting with Mao 13 January 1967. [source: LIU]
- 31 October 1968: 12th Plenum of the 8th CC expelled Liu "forever" as "traitor, spy and scab"; by then 71% of the 8th CC had been labelled traitors, spies or anti-party; only one attendee (Chen Shaomin) dissented. [source: LIU]
- **Liu's death**: held in solitary confinement in Zhongnanhai, gravely ill (pneumonia, diabetes); flown to Kaifeng, Henan, on 17 October 1969; died 12 November 1969 aged 70; cremated secretly under the name "Liu Weihuang" as a supposed contagious patient. His physician Gu Qihua later disputed claims of medical neglect. Rehabilitated 24 February 1980; state memorial 17 May 1980 (Deng gave the eulogy); ashes scattered at sea. [source: LIU] — **CR says Liu got a "belated state funeral" at the Dec 1978 plenum and was "sent to a detention camp"; LIU (more detailed) gives 1980 and Zhongnanhai/Kaifeng — use LIU.**
- Wang Guangmei jailed 1967, spent 12 years in prison. [source: LIU]
- **Deng Xiaoping**: denounced as the "number two capitalist roader"; purged twice (1966–67 and April 1976). Son Deng Pufang, held by Red Guards at Peking University, fell or was thrown from a four-storey window in 1968 and became paraplegic. October 1969 Deng sent to the Xinjian County Tractor Factory, Jiangxi, as a fitter (about four years). [source: DENG]
- Peng Dehuai: seized by Red Guards 1966, repeatedly struggled and beaten; died in custody 29 November 1974. He Long: died under house arrest 9 June 1969, denied diabetes medicine. [source: PENG; HE]
- February 1967 "February Countercurrent": marshals Ye Jianying, Chen Yi and vice-premier Tan Zhenlin protested; Mao denounced them (18 Feb). [source: CR]
- Zhang Zhixin, party member who criticised the Mao cult and the case against Liu: imprisoned 1969–75, executed 4 April 1975; reportedly her larynx was cut before execution. [source: ZHG; LIU]

## 6. Power seizures, factional war, the army (1967–68)
- January Storm, Shanghai (January 1967): rebels including Wang Hongwen, backed by Zhang Chunqiao and Yao Wenyuan, overthrew the municipal government and set up the Shanghai People's Commune; within three weeks 24 more province-level governments overthrown; replaced by "revolutionary committees". [source: CR]
- PLA ordered to "support the left" (January 1967); "Three Supports and Two Militaries" (March 1967). [source: CR]
- **Wuhan incident (20 July 1967)**: conservative "Million Heroes" (c. 500,000; backed by Wuhan Military Region commander Chen Zaidao) vs radical "Workers' Headquarters". Mao secretly in Wuhan; Unit 8201 and Million Heroes seized CRG emissaries Wang Li and Xie Fuzhi; Wang Li beaten (leg broken). Mao flown out to Shanghai; Chen Zaidao later removed (and later rehabilitated). Considered the first time military leaders defied central orders. Estimated ~1,000 killed in Wuhan in July 1967 (uncited in WUH); 184,000 "seized, beaten, maimed or killed" province-wide. [source: WUH; CR]
- After Wuhan, Jiang Qing's slogan "attack with words, defend with weapons" (文攻武卫) was taken as licence for armed struggle. [source: WUH]
- Weapons in civilian hands: CR cites 18.8 million firearms, 14,828 artillery pieces, 2.72 million grenades (a note says "some claim 1.877 million" guns) — **figure disputed; say "millions of firearms"**. Tanks and warships used in Chongqing, Xiamen, Changchun. Violent struggles killed an estimated 300,000–500,000. [source: CR]
- Industrial output fell 14% in 1967. [source: CR]
- Walder: casualties peaked not in the 1967 insurgency but in the 1968 restoration of order — "The cure for factional warfare was far worse than the disease." [source: CR]
- July 1968: workers' "Mao Zedong Thought Propaganda Team" entered Tsinghua University (27 July) to end campus fighting; 28 July Mao met five Beijing Red Guard leaders: "The Black Hand is nobody else but me!" Mango cult followed (mangoes from Pakistan's foreign minister, 4 Aug 1968). [source: CR]
- Cleansing the Class Ranks (1968–69): at least 500,000 deaths (CR); One Strike–Three Anti campaign (1970): 1.87 million persecuted, over 284,800 arrested or killed Feb–Nov 1970 (government statistics). [source: CR]
- Chongqing: Shapingba Red Guard cemetery, the only large surviving graveyard of factional dead (over 400 buried per China Daily 2010), normally locked; opened for relatives at Qingming. [source: https://covid-19.chinadaily.com.cn/china/2010-04/08/content_9699494.htm (China Daily 2010, via search); https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2016/04/05/a-corpse-commander-shares-tales-of-burying-cultural-revolution-dead/1093951]

## 7. Massacres and ethnic minorities
- **Guangxi**: lynchings and massacres 1967–68 between "United Headquarters" (conservative, backing Wei Guoqing) and "4.22" (rebel); final stage summer 1968 when Guangzhou Military Region troops (Huang Yongsheng) attacked Nanning and Guilin. Official 1984 investigation: 89,810 documented abnormal deaths plus nearly 20,000 missing → "over 100,000"; other estimates 70,400 to 150,000–500,000. Cannibalism: at least 137 eaten (initial records), 302 (official archives), 421 named victims (independent researchers), notably in Wuxuan County (at least 38 eaten). Archives compiled 1986–88 and published in the US in 2016 (*Secret Archives about the Cultural Revolution in Guangxi*). [source: GX]
- Walder calls Guangxi a "regional civil war" organised through the militia and military command; Yang Su (Su Yang) instead proposes a "community model". [source: GX]
- **Dao County (Daoxian), Hunan**: 7,696 killed 13 Aug–17 Oct 1967, plus 1,397 forced suicides. [source: CR]
- Chinese scholars estimate at least 300,000 died in massacres; Guangxi county annals: 43 counties record massacres, 15 with over 1,000 dead. [source: CR; GX]
- **Inner Mongolia ("Inner Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party" purge, 1967–69)**, led by Lt-Gen Teng Haiqing after Ulanhu's fall (Aug 1966): official 1980 Supreme People's Procuratorate figures — 346,000 arrested (75% Mongols), 16,222 persecuted to death, 81,000+ permanently disabled. Other estimates: 20,000–100,000 dead (Ba He: nearly 100,000; Song Yongyi: at least 40,000; Lhamjab Borjigin: at least 27,900). CR's figures (790,000 persecuted, 22,900 beaten to death, 120,000 maimed) differ again. Mongolian language banned from publications. Teng was never tried. Lhamjab Borjigin was prosecuted in 2019 for his research. [source: IMI; CR] — **sources disagree; give official figure and range.**
- **Tibet**: Monlam festival banned Feb 1966; Red Guards (mainly Tibetan students from Beijing) arrived November 1966; factions Gyenlo ("Rebels") vs Nyamdre ("Alliance"); PLA attacked the Gyenlo stronghold in the Jokhang in June 1968 (12 militants, 2 soldiers killed); Nyemo 1969 revolt around the nun Trinley Chödrön. Of over 6,000 monasteries, only eight were intact by the end of the 1970s — destruction began after 1959, before the Cultural Revolution, often with local Tibetan Red Guard participation. Debate: Wang Lixiong stresses authorities' restraint and Tibetan participation; Tsering Shakya replies that this blames the victims. [source: TIB; CR]
- Others: Xinjiang (Qur'ans burned), Yanbian Koreans, Shadian incident (Yunnan, 1975: PLA killed over 1,600 Hui, including 300 children). [source: CR]
- Sexual violence against sent-down women documented by Pan Suiming, Emily Honig and others. [source: CR]

## 8. Sent-down youth
- 22 December 1968 *People's Daily* published Mao's instruction that urban youth "go to the villages to be reeducated by poor peasants". [source: UPD]
- Numbers: about 17 million urban youths sent down 1968–1979 (CR) / "approximately 17 million" in total (UPD); CR's lead says "over 10 million". Use "some 17 million". Policy ended 1978; mass protests (notably Xishuangbanna) led the State Council to allow return in early 1979. [source: UPD; CR]
- Motives: ideological re-education, dispersing the Red Guards from cities, urban unemployment. Called the "lost generation". [source: UPD; CR]
- Xi Jinping: father Xi Zhongxun purged (1962/63); in 1969 Xi was sent down to Liangjiahe, Shaanxi, lived in a cave dwelling (yaodong) about seven years; half-sister Xi Heping took her own life; entered Tsinghua 1975 as a worker-peasant-soldier student. [source: XI]
- Wang Youqin herself was sent down to Yunnan. [source: WYQ]

## 9. Lin Biao, 1969–1971
- 9th Party Congress, April 1969: Lin written into the party constitution as Mao's "closest comrade-in-arms and successor"; military over 28% of the new CC. [source: CR]
- Sino-Soviet border clashes at the Ussuri (Zhenbao), March 1969; Lin's "Order Number One" (18 Oct 1969) angered Mao. Lushan plenum (Aug 1970): fight over restoring the post of state chairman; Chen Boda purged. [source: CR]
- "Project 571" (homophone for "armed uprising"): coup outline attributed to Lin's son Lin Liguo (Air Force officer). Whether Lin Biao himself was involved is disputed (Jin Qiu portrays him as passive). [source: CR; LINI]
- **13 September 1971**: Trident 1E (PLAAF 256) from Shanhaiguan carrying Lin Biao, wife Ye Qun, Lin Liguo and others crashed near Öndörkhaan, Mongolia; all nine aboard killed. Daughter Lin Liheng ("Doudou") had warned security. Official version: fleeing to USSR after failed plot, ran out of fuel. Soviet investigation confirmed Lin's death but found the aircraft had enough fuel; a 1994 Western investigation found the plane flying *away* from the USSR when it crashed. Many records destroyed in the late 1970s. News withheld from the public for about two months; National Day celebrations cancelled. [source: LINI; CR] — **disputed; present official version with caveats.**
- Effect: disillusionment; Mao's authority damaged. [source: CR]

## 10. Gang of Four and the end, 1972–1976
- Gang of Four: Jiang Qing, Zhang Chunqiao, Yao Wenyuan, Wang Hongwen (Shanghai worker made party vice-chairman 1973). [source: GOF; CR]
- Rapprochement with the US (Nixon, 1972); "Criticize Lin, Criticize Confucius" campaign (1973–74), partly aimed at Zhou Enlai. [source: CR]
- Deng restored as vice-premier March 1973; ran government after January 1975; attacked again late 1975 ("Counterattack the Right-Deviationist Reversal-of-Verdicts Trend"). [source: CR]
- Zhou Enlai died 8 January 1976; Hua Guofeng made premier. [source: CR; TAM]
- **Tiananmen (April Fifth) incident**: 4–5 April 1976 around Qingming; mourners laid wreaths and poems at the Monument to the People's Heroes; up to two million may have visited on 4 April; tributes removed overnight; on 5 April riot (police cars burned); square cleared that night by militia and security forces; Deng blamed and stripped of all posts 7 April. Similar incidents in Nanjing and other cities. Verdict reversed as "revolutionary" in November–December 1978. [source: TAM; CR]
- Mao died 9 September 1976 (Tangshan earthquake of July 1976 not verified here). Gang of Four arrested 6 October 1976 by Unit 8341 in a bloodless coup organised by Hua Guofeng with Ye Jianying, Wang Dongxing (and Li Xiannian, Chen Yun per CR). Usually taken as the end of the Cultural Revolution. [source: GOF; CR]
- Hua's "Two Whatevers"; Deng back by July 1977; Third Plenum December 1978 begins reform and opening. Boluan Fanzheng ("setting things right"): over 3 million "unjust, false and erroneous" cases redressed. [source: CR]

## 11. Trial and verdicts
- **Trial** of the Gang of Four (and Lin Biao group) by a special court of the Supreme People's Court, November 1980–January 1981 (verdict 25 January 1981), televised. Jiang Qing defiant, the only one to argue her own case — she obeyed Mao; Zhang Chunqiao silent; Yao and Wang confessed. Sentences: Jiang Qing and Zhang Chunqiao death with two-year reprieve (commuted to life); Wang Hongwen life; Yao Wenyuan 20 years. Jiang Qing killed herself 14 May 1991; Wang died 1992; Yao and Zhang 2005. [source: GOF; JQ]
- Persecution figures in the indictment: CR says 729,511 persecuted, 34,800 died; GOF says ~750,000 persecuted, 34,375 died. [source: CR; GOF] — **minor discrepancy; say "about 730,000–750,000 persecuted and some 34,000 deaths".**
- Jiang Qing's line "I was Chairman Mao's dog. Whomever he asked me to bite, I bit" — widely attributed, wording varies, no verbatim transcript found (see Quotations / NOT VERIFIED).
- **1981 Resolution** ("Resolution on Certain Questions in the History of Our Party since the Founding of the PRC"), adopted unanimously 27 June 1981 at the 6th Plenum of the 11th CC (same plenum accepted Hua's resignation as chairman); drafting led by Hu Qiaomu over ~15 months, steered by Deng, discussed by 4,000 cadres. Key lines: "Chief responsibility for the grave 'Left' error of the 'Cultural Revolution,' an error comprehensive in magnitude and protracted in duration, does indeed lie with Comrade Mao Zedong"; the CR was "responsible for the most severe setback and the heaviest losses suffered by the Party, the country, and the people since the founding of the People's Republic"; blame diluted onto the Lin Biao and Jiang Qing "counter-revolutionary cliques"; Mao's "merits are primary and his errors secondary". "Mao 70% right, 30% wrong" is attributed to Deng but does not appear in the text. [source: RES; EVAL; CR]
- Deng to Oriana Fallaci (August 1980): Mao's "mistakes were only secondary". [source: RES]
- 2021 third historical resolution (Xi era) repeats that Mao launched the CR, calling it a "catastrophe" with the most serious setback since 1949 — some outlets say it addresses the period only lightly. [source: https://amp.scmp.com/news/china/politics/article/3156299/cultural-revolution-was-catastrophe-and-mao-was-responsible; https://www.rfa.org/english/news/china/cult-11172021091215.html]

## 12. The human toll (estimates and who gives them)
- No agreed figure; estimates "range from hundreds of thousands to millions"; CR lead uses "1 to 2 million". [source: CR]
- **Walder & Su (2003, *China Quarterly*, "The Cultural Revolution in the Countryside: Scope, Timing and Human Impact")**: from 1,520 county annals, rural deaths 750,000–1.5 million; about 36 million politically persecuted; similar number permanently injured; most casualties 1968–71. [source: https://aparc.fsi.stanford.edu/node/24671 (via search)]
- **MacFarquhar & Schoenhals, *Mao's Last Revolution* (2006)**: per Publishers Weekly's review, they cite (conservatively) 36 million persecuted in rural China, 750,000–1.5 million killed, roughly the same number permanently injured — i.e. they adopt the Walder–Su figure. [source: https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780674023321 (via search)]
- **Walder (2014 paper; *Agents of Disorder*, Harvard 2019)**: from 2,213 county annals read conservatively, 1.1–1.6 million deaths for 1966–71; insurgents caused about a quarter; most deaths came from state repression restoring order. [source: CR; https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2019/10/violence-unfolded-chinas-cultural-revolution]
- **Yang Su**, *Collective Killings in Rural China during the Cultural Revolution* (Cambridge UP 2011; 2012 ASA Barrington Moore Award): 1,500+ county gazetteers, focus Guangxi and Guangdong; "community model". (No single national figure verified for Su.) [source: YS]
- Internal party figures (1978/1984 investigations, cited by CCP History Research Center book, 1996 per CR): 1.728 million "unnatural deaths", 4.2 million detained, 1.3 million arrested, 135,000 executed as counter-revolutionaries, 237,000 killed and 7.03 million disabled in violent struggles; CR says scholars find these "very reasonable". [source: CR]
- Other estimates: AFP 1979 — 400,000 (Meisner: "unlikely that it was less"); Ding Shu (1999) — 2 million; Rummel (1991) — 7.73 million incl. labour camps; Ye Jianying's alleged "20 million" — discredited (Jie Li). [source: CR]
- Walder (1980s): the field now sees it as "a human disaster, even a historical crime". [source: EVAL]

## 13. Memory and its management today
- New Enlightenment / scar literature: genre of the late 1970s; Lu Xinhua's story "Scar" (1978) gave the name; Liu Xinwu's "The Class Monitor" (1977) also called a pioneer; Chen Ruoxi's "The Execution of Mayor Yin" (1974, Hong Kong) earliest exemplar. Films criticising the CR later banned as "historical nihilism". [source: SCAR; EVAL; HS]
- Ba Jin called for a Cultural Revolution museum ("Let history not be repeated" must not be an empty phrase). [source: EVAL]
- **Shantou (Chenghai) Cultural Revolution Museum**: in Tashan park, founded by former Shantou vice-mayor Peng Qi'an (himself struggled 30+ times), opened 1 January 2005 as the only CR museum on the mainland; inscribed the 1981 verdict at its entrance; one mural listed over 4,000 local dead; annual 8 August memorial 2006–2013; donations account closed 2013; handed to local government 2015; closed and covered up late April 2016, ahead of the 50th anniversary. ## Debates
- **Motives**: power struggle vs ideology vs legacy project; MacFarquhar & Schoenhals combine them; Karl stresses revolution as end; Jin Qiu sees the movement escaping Mao's vision into many "local revolutions". [source: EVAL]
- **Top-down vs bottom-up violence**: Walder — most killing was organised repression by state/army/militia restoring order ("regional civil war" in Guangxi); Song Yongyi — massacres were acts of "state apparatuses"; Yang Su — "community model" in which local communities killed under indirect state impetus. [source: GX; YS; CR]
- **Death toll**: hundreds of thousands to ~2 million (scholarly); higher numbers (Rummel, "20 million") rejected by most. [source: CR]
- **Revisionist/positive views**: Gao Mobo (benefits to rural workers, egalitarianism); Yiching Wu (the "era of madness" narrative flattens complexity and serves to delegitimise China's revolution) vs mainstream view (Thurston, Barnouin & Yu, Walder) of a human disaster. [source: EVAL]
- **Lin Biao**: official coup-and-flight story vs Western doubts about his role, the fuel and the flight path. [source: LINI]
- **Tibet**: Wang Lixiong vs Tsering Shakya on Tibetan participation and blame. [source: TIB]
- **Bian Zhongyun**: responsibility of Song Binbin (Wang Youqin) vs her denial; later naming of Deng Rong and Liu Pingping by others as among the students involved (claims, not proven). [source: BIAN; SGB]
