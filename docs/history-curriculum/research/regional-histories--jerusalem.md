## Bronze Age origins and the Jebusites
- First settlement near the Gihon Spring is dated by archaeological evidence to c. 3000–2800 BCE. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- The first known mention of the city is in Egyptian Middle Kingdom "execration texts" of c. 2000 BCE (rendered *Rusalimum*). The root S-L-M is thought to refer to "peace" or to Shalim, a Canaanite god of dusk/the setting sun. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- In the Amarna letters (1330s BCE), Jerusalem's ruler Abdi-Heba (also Abdi-Kheba) wrote to the pharaoh as a vassal; six letters (EA 285–290) are his. He complains of attacks by rival Canaanite rulers and bands of 'Apiru/Habiru, and asks for archers. The city is written *Urusalim*. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi-Heba ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebusites]
- Abdi-Heba's name means "servant of Hebat", a Hurrian goddess. Estimates suggest the area he ruled may have had about 1,500 people and that 14th-century BCE Jerusalem was a "small highlands stronghold" (caveat: an estimate cited by Wikipedia; fortification is disputed — another Wikipedia article says Canaanites had built massive walls by the 17th century BCE, flagged there as needing better sources). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi-Heba ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- The Hebrew Bible is the only ancient text that calls the pre-Israelite inhabitants "Jebusites" and the city "Jebus" (1 Chronicles 11:4). Niels Peter Lemche disputes the identification of Jebus with Jerusalem, noting that no non-biblical source uses "Jebus". Scholars variously see the Jebusites as Hittite, Hurrian-influenced, or (Edward Lipiński) an Amorite group. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebusites]
- 2 Samuel says the Jebusites taunted that even "the blind and the lame" could hold off David; the Masoretic text says David's men took the city via a "water shaft", but the Septuagint reads "with their daggers", and archaeologists consider a secret entry via the heavily defended Gihon spring implausible. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebusites]
- The Bible says David bought a threshing floor from the Jebusite Araunah and built an altar there, which the Books of Chronicles equate with the site of Solomon's Temple; some biblical scholars read this as giving an Israelite foundation to an older sanctuary. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## The City of David and the archaeological debate
- Biblical tradition dates David's capture of Jerusalem to c. 1000 BCE; the Bible places the split of the kingdoms near the end of Solomon's reign, c. 930 BCE. Finkelstein and others dispute that a great united monarchy existed. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- "Minimalism" (the "Copenhagen School") arose in the 1990s, chiefly Niels Peter Lemche and Thomas L. Thompson (Copenhagen), Philip R. Davies and Keith Whitelam; the label was given by opponents, who were dubbed "maximalists" (e.g. William Dever, Iain Provan, Kenneth Kitchen); neither side accepted the labels and most scholars stood in the middle. Some minimalist claims (on the Exodus, conquest, united monarchy) have been absorbed into the mainstream. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_minimalism]
- Thomas L. Thompson argues Jerusalem only became a city able to act as a state capital in the mid-7th century BCE; Omer Sergi argues recent finds in the City of David and Ophel show it was already significant in Iron Age IIA. Radiocarbon dates indicate Jerusalem was relatively densely inhabited in the 12th–10th centuries BCE and began a westward expansion in the 9th century BCE. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Israel Finkelstein's "Low Chronology" redates buildings once attributed to Solomon to the 9th-century Omride kings of the northern kingdom; he accepts a historical David and Solomon in the 10th century BCE as founders of a dynasty but sees the great united monarchy as an ideological construct of late 7th-century BCE (Josiah-era) writers. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Finkelstein]
- The Stepped Stone Structure ("Area G") is a curved, roughly 18 m (60 ft) high terraced stone structure on the east side of the City of David. Macalister (1920s) thought it Jebusite; Kathleen Kenyon (1960s) dated it to the start of Iron Age II (1000–900 BCE); Yigal Shiloh excavated it in the 1970s–80s; Finkelstein et al. argue its upper part was built or rebuilt in the Hasmonean period; Jodi Magness dates its initial construction to the 12th century BCE. Some identify it with the biblical "Millo". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepped_Stone_Structure]
- Eilat Mazar (1956–2021) announced on 4 August 2005 the "Large Stone Structure", which she interpreted as possibly King David's palace, dating it to the 10th–9th centuries BCE and linking it with the Stepped Stone Structure. Her dig was sponsored by the Shalem Center. Finds include bullae of Jehucal son of Shelemiah and Gedaliah son of Pashhur, officials named in the Book of Jeremiah (late 7th–early 6th c. BCE). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Stone_Structure ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat_Mazar]
- In 2007 Finkelstein, Ze'ev Herzog, David Ussishkin and Lily Singer-Avitz argued the remains are not one building and that much is Hellenistic; Finkelstein: "all one can safely say is that its various elements post-date the late Iron I/early Iron IIA and predate the Roman period." Amihai Mazar suggested it might be the Jebusite "fortress of Zion"; Avraham Faust and William Dever support a 10th-century date. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Stone_Structure]
- In 2010 Eilat Mazar announced a stretch of wall she dated to the 10th century BCE ("a regime capable of carrying out such construction"); Aren Maeir was among the doubters. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat_Mazar]

## First Temple, Assyria and Babylon
- Jerusalem was capital of Judah for some 400 years and survived Sennacherib's siege of 701 BCE; Sennacherib's own annals (Taylor Prism) say Hezekiah was shut up "like a caged bird" and paid 30 talents of gold and 800 of silver. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Nebuchadnezzar II's first siege (597 BCE) took King Jehoiachin and much of the elite into captivity. The final siege began in January 589 BCE and the city fell in summer (month of Tammuz/Av) 587 or 586 BCE: scholars disagree (Albright 587, Thiele 586; Rodger Young 2004 argued 587). The Babylonians burned the Temple and walls; Zedekiah's sons were executed before him and he was blinded. Only the Hebrew Bible records the 587/586 siege; the Nebuchadnezzar Chronicle covers 597. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(587_BC) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Jewish tradition mourns the destruction of both Temples on Tisha B'Av (the Ninth of Av). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(587_BC)]

## Second Temple, Hasmoneans and Herod
- Cyrus II allowed exiles to return after 539 BCE. The Second Temple was completed in the sixth year of Darius I (516 BCE) according to Ezra; Wikipedia's Second Temple article gives "either around 516 BCE or between 450 and 400 BCE". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple]
- In 167 BCE Antiochus IV ordered an altar to Zeus in the Temple; Judas Maccabeus retook Jerusalem and restored Temple worship in 164 BCE (commemorated at Hanukkah); Jerusalem became capital of the Hasmonean state. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple]
- Pompey took Jerusalem in 63 BCE and entered the Holy of Holies. Herod the Great captured the city in 37 BCE after a 40-day siege. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Herod began rebuilding the Temple c. 20/19 BCE (Josephus, *Antiquities* XV.380: Herod's 18th year); the main work was completed c. 18 CE per Wikipedia's infobox; he doubled the size of the platform, making it the largest religious enclosure of the ancient world. The Temple Mount platform was created with four massive retaining walls. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- Modern estimates for late Second Temple Jerusalem's population: about 70,000–100,000 (Wikipedia, History of Jerusalem); individual estimates for 70 CE: Wilkinson (1974) 70,398, Broshi (1978) 80,000, Levine (2002) 60,000–70,000. A size not matched again until the 20th century. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Jerusalem]
- After Herod's death (4 BCE), Judea came under direct Roman rule from 6 CE, with Caesarea as administrative capital. Jerusalem was, per the New Testament, the place of Jesus's crucifixion and resurrection and the first Christian community. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## 70 CE, Aelia Capitolina and the Bar Kokhba revolt
- The First Jewish–Roman War (66–73 CE): Titus besieged Jerusalem in spring 70 with at least 48,200 soldiers; rival rebel factions under John of Gischala, Simon bar Giora and Eleazar ben Simon fought one another; in the month of Av (July/August) 70 the Romans took the Temple Mount and destroyed the Temple; the rest of the city fell in following weeks. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(70_CE)]
- Figures (all ancient, contested): Tacitus says 600,000 were besieged; Josephus gives 1.1 million dead and 97,000 captives taken in the whole war. Menahem Stern rejects such totals as exaggerated. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(70_CE)]
- Spoils, including the menorah, were paraded in a triumph in Rome and shown on the Arch of Titus. The surviving retaining walls of the platform include the Western Wall. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(70_CE)]
- Hadrian founded the colony Aelia Capitolina on his visit of 129/130 CE ("Aelia" from his family name; "Capitolina" for Jupiter Capitolinus). Coins struck before the revolt have persuaded most historians that the foundation preceded, and helped provoke, the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135/136), as Cassius Dio said. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelia_Capitolina]
- Cassius Dio gives 580,000 killed in the Bar Kokhba war, with 50 outposts and 985 villages razed. Jews were then banned from the city except, by tradition, on Tisha B'Av; the province Judaea was renamed Syria Palaestina. The Arabic early name for Jerusalem, *Īliyā'*, derives from "Aelia". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Kokhba_revolt ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelia_Capitolina ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## Byzantine Christian Jerusalem
- Bishop Macarius, with Constantine's backing, had a pagan temple removed c. 326 and identified a rock-cut tomb as Jesus's; Helena (Constantine's mother) is credited with finding the True Cross. The Church of the Holy Sepulchre was consecrated on 13 September 335. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre]
- Emperor Julian permitted the Jews to rebuild the Temple in 363; the project failed. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]
- Justinian's Nea Church (New Church of the Theotokos) was consecrated in 543. The 6th-century Madaba Map mosaic is the oldest surviving original map of the Holy Land and Jerusalem, showing the colonnaded Cardo. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaba_Map ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- Burial evidence suggests Byzantine Jerusalem's population was Christian; the Temple Mount was left undeveloped. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]

## The Persian conquest of 614
- Sasanian forces under Shahrbaraz, aided by Jews of Palaestina Prima in revolt against Byzantium, took Jerusalem in April–May 614 after a siege of about 20–21 days. The True Cross was taken to Ctesiphon. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_conquest_of_Jerusalem]
- Christian death tolls vary greatly: the Armenian Sebeos gives 17,000; manuscripts of the monk Antiochus Strategos give up to 66,509 (others about half), with 24,518 or 4,518 bodies at the Mamilla pool; the archaeologist Ronny Reich estimated about 60,000. Large-scale destruction of churches has not been confirmed archaeologically, though there is evidence of mass killing. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_conquest_of_Jerusalem]
- Jewish leaders briefly held roles in the city (614–617) before the Sasanians reversed course; Heraclius re-entered Jerusalem with the True Cross on 21 March 630. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_conquest_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## Muslim conquest (637/638)
- After the Byzantine defeat at Yarmuk (August 636), Abu Ubayda's forces besieged Jerusalem from November 636; Patriarch Sophronius surrendered on condition he submit only to the caliph. Caliph Umar came in person in 637 or 638; academic sources tend to prefer 638. The siege appears to have been largely bloodless. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Muslim_conquest_of_Jerusalem]
- Umar's "Assurance" (al-'Uhda al-'Umariyya) guaranteed Christians' lives, property and churches in exchange for the *jizya* tax. Muslim chronicles say Umar declined Sophronius's invitation to pray in the Holy Sepulchre so Muslims would not later claim it as a mosque (a later tradition). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Muslim_conquest_of_Jerusalem]
- Umar permitted Jews to live in the city again. The Gallic pilgrim Arculf (c. 679–682) described a rough wooden house of prayer on the Temple Mount holding up to 3,000. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Muslim_conquest_of_Jerusalem]
- The Qur'an (Sura 17:1) refers to Muhammad's Night Journey to "the farthest place of prayer" (*al-masjid al-aqsa*); Jerusalem is not named in the Qur'an, but hadith and tradition identify it; Muslims initially prayed towards Jerusalem until the qibla was changed to Mecca 16 or 17 months after the Hijra. The Arabic names include Bayt al-Maqdis / al-Quds ("the Holy"). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## The Dome of the Rock and al-Aqsa
- The Dome of the Rock was built by the Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik over the Foundation Stone; its dedicatory Kufic inscription is dated AH 72 (691/692 CE). It is the oldest surviving work of Islamic architecture and contains the earliest inscriptions proclaiming Islam and Muhammad. Supervisors were Raja ibn Haywa and Yazid ibn Sallam. It is a shrine, not a mosque. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Its octagonal plan and mosaics follow Byzantine models (possibly the Kathisma church, 451–458). The Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun later replaced Abd al-Malik's name in the inscription but left the date. Suleiman the Magnificent replaced the exterior mosaics with Ottoman tiles in the 16th century. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]
- The inscriptions repeat "God has no companion" (*la sharika lahu*) five times and address Jesus as prophet and servant of God, which S. D. Goitein read as polemic against Christianity. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]
- Motives debated: (1) a victory monument asserting Islam against Christianity; (2) an attempt to divert pilgrimage from Mecca, then held by the rival caliph Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr — most modern historians dismiss this as anti-Umayyad propaganda, though some think it cannot be ruled out. Oleg Grabar called it a "unique monument of Islamic culture in almost all respects". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]
- The al-Aqsa (Qibli) Mosque on the south of the compound was built by Abd al-Malik or his son al-Walid I (r. 705–715), or both; the Aphrodito papyri (708–711) mention Egyptian workers sent for the "Mosque of Jerusalem". It was rebuilt repeatedly after earthquakes; the Fatimid caliph al-Zahir reduced it from 15 aisles to 7 in 1034–36. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Mosque ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Terminology: "al-Masjid al-Aqsa" in Muslim usage is the whole compound; "Jami' al-Aqsa"/"Qibli Mosque" the prayer hall. The Jerusalem Islamic Waqf calls "Temple Mount" a "newly-created Judaization term"; the PLO in 2014 urged journalists not to use it. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Mosque ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- The Fatimid caliph al-Hakim ordered the Holy Sepulchre destroyed on 18 October 1009 (part of a wider campaign against churches and synagogues); it was rebuilt by 1048 under a Byzantine–Fatimid agreement. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre]
- The Seljuk commander Atsiz took Jerusalem in 1071; the Fatimids retook it in August 1098, shortly before the crusaders arrived. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## Crusader kingdom (1099) and Saladin (1187)
- Siege 7 June – 15 July 1099; the crusaders broke in on Friday 15 July. Godfrey of Bouillon was chosen ruler on 22 July 1099 as "Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre", reportedly refusing a crown of gold where Christ wore a crown of thorns; his brother Baldwin was crowned king on Christmas Day 1100 at Bethlehem. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1099) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Massacre figures (attribute): Fulcher of Chartres said 10,000 were killed in the Temple area alone; later Arab sources claim 70,000 or even 100,000 dead, which Wikipedia, citing Thomas Madden, calls unrealistic for a population of about 30,000 (Benjamin Kedar put the population at about 20,000–30,000 — search snippet). Modern estimates: 3,000–10,000 killed. Jewish defenders were burned in their synagogue according to several accounts, though a Cairo Geniza letter written about two weeks later mentions the synagogue burning without people inside. Eyewitness accounts do not mention killing of Eastern Christians, many of whom the Fatimid governor had expelled before the siege. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1099) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_Jerusalem_(1099) ; search snippet: https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Talk:Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1099)]
- Christopher Tyerman: a "sustained massacre in the hysteria of success, followed three days' later by more cold-blooded mass killing." [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_Jerusalem_(1099)]
- Baldwin I reinstated the ban on Jewish residence; Jewish life in the city nearly ceased in the 12th century. The crusaders called the Dome of the Rock *Templum Domini* (made a church) and al-Aqsa *Templum Solomonis* (royal palace, then from 1119 headquarters of the Knights Templar). The Holy Sepulchre was rebuilt as a Romanesque church. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Mosque]
- Benjamin of Tudela (1173) found about 200 Jews living under the Tower of David. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- After Hattin (4 July 1187) Saladin besieged Jerusalem 20 September – 2 October 1187; Balian of Ibelin negotiated surrender. Ransom for Franks was 10 dinars (per man in Wikipedia's account); Balian paid 30,000 dinars to free 7,000 poor; al-Adil freed 1,000, Heraclius 700, Balian 500, and the aged were freed; some thousands who could not pay were enslaved (Wikipedia infobox: 11,000). Imad al-Din's triumphal account boasted of women and children divided as captives. Native Christians could stay; Jews were allowed to resettle. Saladin's surrender of the city was "relatively peaceful especially in contrast" to 1099. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1187)]
- Saladin removed crusader installations from al-Aqsa within a week, installed Nur al-Din's minbar (made 1168 in Damascus; destroyed in the 1969 arson), and a 583 AH/1187 inscription commemorates the restoration. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Mosque]
- Muslim families keep the keys and door of the Holy Sepulchre: the Joudeh al-Goudia family holds the key; the Nuseibeh family opens the door — by documents since Saladin's time (family tradition claims the 7th century). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre]
- In 1219 the Ayyubid al-Mu'azzam razed the walls; Frederick II held the city by treaty from 1229; the Khwarazmians sacked it in 1244. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Saladin's son al-Afdal endowed the Maghribi (Moroccan) Quarter beside the Western Wall as a *waqf* in 1193 (per Mujir al-Din). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughrabi_Quarter]

## Mamluk and Ottoman Jerusalem
- The Mamluks ruled Jerusalem from 1260 to 1516; about 90 Mamluk structures (13th–15th c.) survive — madrasas, hospices, sabils, markets — clustered around the Haram; e.g. Tankiz's Cotton Market (1336–7) and Qaytbay's Madrasa al-Ashrafiyya (1482). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Franciscans bought the Cenacle c. 1333; Pope Clement VI created the Custody of the Holy Land in 1342. Nahmanides is traditionally said to have founded a synagogue in 1267 (tradition of doubtful authenticity). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- The Ottomans took Jerusalem in 1516 (the wider Mamluk conquest 1516–17). Suleiman ordered the walls rebuilt in 1535; they were built c. 1537–1541 (other Wikipedia articles say 1535–1542), about 4 km long (4,018 m; another article says about 4.5 km), averaging 12 m high, with 34–35 towers and seven open gates; attributed to the office of Mimar Sinan. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_of_Jerusalem]
- Ottoman tax registers (Cohen and Lewis): 1525–26 about 5,612 inhabitants (1,194 Jews, 3,704 Muslims, 714 Christians); 1553–54 about 16,068 (1,958 Jews, 12,154 Muslims, 1,956 Christians). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Jerusalem]
- Suleiman's era is said to have created the narrow prayer space at the Western Wall between it and the Maghribi Quarter houses. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughrabi_Quarter]
- Judah HeHasid's group arrived in 1700 and began the Hurva synagogue, destroyed in 1721 over debts, rebuilt from 1837/1864. Local notables revolted in 1703–1705 (Naqib al-Ashraf revolt). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurva_Synagogue]

## 19th century: consuls, the Status Quo, and growth
- Britain was the first European power to open a consulate in Jerusalem (1838 or 1839; William Tanner Young first vice-consul); France followed in 1843 (search snippet). [source: search snippet: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tanner_Young ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_consuls-general_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_Jerusalem]
- Jerusalem district was placed under Constantinople in 1841 and made an independent Mutasarrifate in 1872, including Hebron, Jaffa, Gaza and Beersheba. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarrifate_of_Jerusalem]
- The Status Quo rests on a firman of Osman III (1757) dividing rights in Christian holy places; firmans of 1852 and 1853 (Abdülmecid I) froze arrangements so no change could be made without consensus; it was recognised by the Treaty of Paris (1856) and named in Article 62 of the Treaty of Berlin (1878). The Franco-Russian quarrel over the holy places was one cause of the Crimean War. L. G. A. Cust's 1929 summary became the standard text. It covers nine sites in Jerusalem and Bethlehem (incl. Holy Sepulchre, Deir es-Sultan, Tomb of the Virgin, Church of the Nativity, Chapel of the Ascension, Western Wall, Rachel's Tomb). The "immovable ladder" on the Holy Sepulchre façade has stood in place since at least the 18th century. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_Quo_(Jerusalem_and_Bethlehem)]
- Population estimates (attribute each; they conflict): 1806 Seetzen 8,774 (2,000 Jews, 4,000 Muslims, 2,774 Christians); 1844 Prussian consul E.-G. Schultz 15,510 (7,120 Jews, 5,000 Muslims, 3,390 Christians); 1872 Ottoman salname 14,358 (3,780 Jews, 6,150 Muslims, 4,428 Christians); 1896 about 45,420 (28,112 Jews). Estimates conflict on whether Jews or Muslims were the plurality in 1838–76 and on when Jews became a majority (between 1882 and 1922). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Jerusalem]
- Building outside the walls began in the 1860s: the Russian Compound and the Jewish Mishkenot Sha'ananim (opposite Zion Gate). About 150 Yemenite Jewish families arrived in 1882 and settled in Silwan from 1884. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- The old city's four "quarters" (Muslim, Christian, Jewish, Armenian) were a loose pattern formalised in the British Mandate. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Rashid Khalidi's *Palestinian Identity* (1997) has a chapter "Competing and Overlapping Loyalties in Ottoman Jerusalem", using Jerusalem notables Yusuf Diya' al-Khalidi and Ruhi al-Khalidi to show religious, Ottoman, local and family loyalties coexisting (search snippet). [source: search snippet: https://digitalprojects.palestine-studies.org/jps/fulltext/40477]

## British Mandate (1917–1948)
- The Ottomans surrendered Jerusalem on 9 December 1917; General Edmund Allenby entered the city on foot on 11 December 1917 and pledged to protect the holy places of "the three religions" (quotation below). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Wall]
- Military governor Ronald Storrs required new buildings to be faced with local stone. Hebrew University (Mount Scopus) and Hadassah hospital were founded. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- Nebi Musa riots, 4–7 April 1920: 5 Jews and 4 Arabs killed. 1929 riots (Western Wall / al-Buraq uprising), 23–29 August, over access to the Western Wall: 133 Jews killed by Arabs (incl. at Hebron and Safed) and 116 Arabs killed, mostly by British police. The Shaw Commission blamed Arab fears of Zionism's consequences. A 1930 international commission confirmed Muslim ownership of the Wall with Jewish rights of access/prayer. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Nebi_Musa_riots ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Palestine_riots ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Wall]
- Census figures (British): 1922 62,578 (33,971 Jews, 13,413 Muslims, 14,669 Christians); 1931 90,053 (51,200 Jews); 1944 c. 157,000 (97,000 Jews, 30,600 Muslims, 29,400 Christians). History of Jerusalem gives 52,000 (1922) to 165,000 (1948), about two-thirds Jews. Note: figures depend on municipal boundaries. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- In July 1946 the Irgun bombed the King David Hotel (British HQ), killing 91 (Partition chapter: "more than 90"). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- UN General Assembly resolution 181 (29 November 1947): "The City of Jerusalem shall be established as a corpus separatum under a special international regime and shall be administered by the United Nations", with a review and referendum after ten years. Accepted by the Jewish leadership, rejected by the Arab leadership. The idea grew from Vatican and Catholic-power concern for the Christian holy places. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_separatum_(Jerusalem) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem]

## 1948 and the divided city (1948–1967)
- From early 1948 Arab forces under Abd al-Qadir al-Husayni blockaded the Tel Aviv–Jerusalem road; Jewish West Jerusalem was rationed (200 g bread) under Dov Yosef; Operation Nachshon (April) briefly broke the blockade; Israel later built the "Burma Road". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Jerusalem]
- 13 April 1948: Arab forces ambushed the Hadassah convoy to Mount Scopus, killing 79 (78 Jewish doctors, nurses, patients, staff and Haganah fighters, plus a British soldier); this followed Deir Yassin (9 April). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadassah_medical_convoy_massacre]
- January 1948: the Haganah bombed the Semiramis Hotel in Katamon, killing 26 civilians; Arab neighbourhoods of West Jerusalem (Katamon, Talbiya, Baka, Mamilla, Abu Tor, Lifta, Ein Karem, Malha, Deir Yassin) were emptied. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- The Jewish Quarter's defenders surrendered to the Arab Legion on 28 May 1948; its roughly 1,500 Jewish residents were expelled to the west. The Arab Legion blew up the Hurva synagogue on 27 May 1948. Displaced Palestinians from Israeli-held Jerusalem: figures range from about 30,000 from the western neighbourhoods (Rashid Khalidi) to 70,000 for the Jerusalem area (as cited in Wikipedia). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurva_Synagogue ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughrabi_Quarter]
- Count Folke Bernadotte, UN mediator, was assassinated in Jerusalem by Lehi in September 1948; US consul Thomas Wasson was killed in May 1948. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- 1949 armistice: Israel held West Jerusalem, Jordan the Old City and East Jerusalem (except an Israeli enclave on Mount Scopus). Israel declared Jerusalem its capital (Knesset resolution 23 January 1950); Jordan annexed East Jerusalem with the West Bank in 1950. Neither was widely recognised. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_annexation_of_East_Jerusalem]
- Under Jordan, Jews were barred from the Old City and the Western Wall for 19 years, despite Article 8 of the 1949 armistice providing for a committee on access; the Mount of Olives cemetery was damaged. Gerald Steinberg (an Israeli political scientist; attribute) says 57 synagogues and study centres were ransacked and 12 destroyed. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Wall ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]

## 1967 and after
- On 5 June 1967 Jordan began shelling Israeli Jerusalem (after King Hussein ignored an Israeli message via UN's Odd Bull); Israeli forces (incl. Mordechai Gur's 55th Paratroopers, Ammunition Hill) took East Jerusalem and the Old City on 7 June 1967. 20 Israeli civilians were killed in Jerusalem in the war. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_annexation_of_East_Jerusalem]
- On the night of 10–11 June 1967 Israel demolished the Mughrabi Quarter to create the Western Wall plaza: about 135 homes housing some 650 people and two mosques among 14 religious buildings (History of Jerusalem). Responsibility is attributed variously to Teddy Kollek, Moshe Dayan, Uzi Narkiss and Shlomo Lahat. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughrabi_Quarter]
- On 27 June 1967 Israel extended its municipal boundary to take in about 70 km² of West Bank land: Jordanian East Jerusalem (6 km²) plus 28 villages (64 km²). Palestinians were offered permanent residency (most declined citizenship). Since 1995 Israel has revoked residency of East Jerusalemites unable to prove their "center of life" is in the city. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_annexation_of_East_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jerusalem]
- 17 June 1967: Dayan met Muslim authorities at al-Aqsa: the Waqf kept religious administration; Jews could visit but not pray; the Western Wall became the Jewish prayer site — the modern "status quo". Israel's Chief Rabbinate ruled Jews should not enter the Mount (sanctity of the Holy of Holies). Israel passed the Preservation of the Holy Places Law (1967). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- Israel declared Jerusalem Day (Yom Yerushalayim); many Israeli Jews celebrated "reunification"/"liberation", with over 200,000 visiting the Western Wall within days. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- 21 August 1969: Denis Michael Rohan, an Australian evangelical Christian, set fire to al-Aqsa, destroying Saladin's minbar; the arson spurred the Rabat summit and the founding of the Organisation of the Islamic Conference. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Mosque]
- 1982: a gunman killed 2 Palestinians at the Dome of the Rock; plots by Jewish extremists to blow up the Dome and al-Aqsa (groups led by Yoel Lerner in 1974, 1977 and 1983; the B'nei Yehuda cult in 1984) were foiled. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]

## Basic Law 1980 and international positions
- "Basic Law: Jerusalem, Capital of Israel" passed the Knesset on 30 July 1980 (a private bill by Geulah Cohen), declaring "Jerusalem, complete and united" the capital; a 2000 amendment barred transferring authority to a foreign body; a 2018 law requires a two-thirds Knesset majority to cede any part. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Law ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem]
- UN Security Council Resolution 478 (20 August 1980), 14–0 with the US abstaining, declared the law "null and void" and called on states to withdraw embassies; 13 states moved embassies to Tel Aviv. The UN and EU regard East Jerusalem as occupied territory; the UN has never revoked the corpus separatum principle; the EU envisages Jerusalem as capital of both states. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_478 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem]
- The PLO's 1988 Declaration of Independence names Jerusalem the capital of Palestine; Jordan in 1988 relinquished West Bank claims except its role at the Muslim holy places. The 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty recognises Jordan's special role / custodianship at the Muslim holy sites. The 2017 OIC declaration called East Jerusalem the capital of Palestine. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- Oslo (1993) deferred Jerusalem to final-status talks. At Camp David (11–25 July 2000) Clinton, Barak and Arafat failed to agree; Jerusalem and the Temple Mount were among five principal obstacles. Barak was the first Israeli PM to consider dividing the city; Olmert later supported detaching Arab neighbourhoods and an international trust for the holy basin. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem]
- The US Congress's Jerusalem Embassy Act (1995) was waived by every president until Donald Trump recognised Jerusalem as Israel's capital on 6 December 2017; the embassy opened on 14 May 2018. The UN General Assembly condemned the move on 21 December 2017 by 128–9 with 35 abstentions. Guatemala (2018), Honduras (2021) and Kosovo followed; Wikipedia (Nov 2026) lists embassies of the US, Fiji, Guatemala, Honduras, Papua New Guinea, Paraguay, Nauru and Colombia plus Kosovo and Somaliland — writer: say "a handful of states" rather than list, as this changes. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_recognition_of_Jerusalem_as_capital_of_Israel ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem]

## Temple Mount / Haram al-Sharif: status and flashpoints
- The Waqf has administered the site since 1187; since 1967 under Hashemite (Jordanian) custodianship with Israeli security control; non-Muslim prayer is banned under the unwritten "status quo". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- 8 October 1990: Israeli Border Police killed about 22 Palestinians during protests sparked by the Temple Mount Faithful's plan to lay a "Third Temple" cornerstone. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- 1996: riots after Netanyahu's government opened an exit to the Western Wall Tunnel in the Muslim Quarter. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem]
- 28 September 2000: Ariel Sharon visited the compound with about 1,000 security personnel ("the Temple Mount is in our hands and will remain in our hands"); clashes followed and the Second (al-Aqsa) Intifada began. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- King Hussein personally paid about $8 million to re-gild the Dome of the Rock (1992–94). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- UNESCO's 26 October 2016 resolution used only Muslim names for the compound, provoking Israeli protest. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- May 2021: protests from 6 May over threatened evictions in Sheikh Jarrah (described by Israel's government as a property dispute and by Palestinians as expulsion), police entry into al-Aqsa on 7 May and Jerusalem Day tensions led to Hamas rocket fire on 10 May and an 11-day Gaza war (ceasefire 21 May). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_crisis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Jarrah_controversy]
- Itamar Ben-Gvir, as National Security Minister, visited the compound on 3 January 2023; Hamas named its 7 October 2023 attack "Operation Al-Aqsa Flood" (cross-refer Modern Conflicts). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks]
- 2024–26 (attribute, keep brief): the Times of Israel reported that Ben-Gvir openly led prayers on the Mount on Tisha B'Av 2024, the first overt ministerial prayer there; by Tisha B'Av (23 July) 2026 police were reported to tolerate Jewish prayer on the eastern side; Jordan's foreign ministry called the visits "a flagrant violation of the historical and legal status quo", while Netanyahu's office said the status quo "has not changed" (search snippets). [source: search snippet: https://timesofisrael.com/in-first-ben-gvir-openly-leads-prayers-on-temple-mount-in-violation-of-status-quo/ ; https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel/religion/artc-thousands-of-jews-visit-temple-mount-for-tisha-b-av-as-ben-gvir-prays-openly]

## Debates
- **Minimalists vs maximalists / the City of David.** Lemche, Thompson, Davies, Whitelam treat the Bible as a late (Persian/Hellenistic) and unreliable source for early Israel; Dever, Provan, Kitchen defend more of it; Finkelstein (Low Chronology) stands between, accepting a 10th-century David as dynastic founder but not a great kingdom; Eilat Mazar and Amihai Mazar read the Large Stone and Stepped Stone Structures as 10th-century monumental building (palace or Jebusite fortress), Finkelstein et al. as multi-period and partly Hellenistic. Note the Shalem Center's sponsorship of Mazar's dig. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_minimalism ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Stone_Structure]
- **Dome of the Rock's purpose.** Anti-Christian victory statement (Goitein's reading of the inscriptions) vs rivalry with Ibn al-Zubayr's Mecca (largely dismissed by modern historians, not wholly excluded). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]
- **Al-Aqsa in the Qur'an.** Some scholars argue the Jerusalem identification of the "farthest mosque" reflects an Umayyad political agenda; Muslim tradition identifies it with Jerusalem from early hadith and Ibn Ishaq. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock]
- **Death tolls of 614 and 1099** depend on later chronicles with inflated, inconsistent numbers (see above). Benjamin Kedar's article "The Jerusalem massacre of 1099 in the western historiography of the crusades" (*Crusades* 3, 2004) surveys how historians have treated the massacre (search snippet). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_conquest_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1099)]
- **Syria Palaestina renaming (c. 135).** Wikipedia calls it the "prevailing scholarly view" that the renaming was punitive; writer should present as a widely held view rather than settled fact. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Kokhba_revolt]
- **Competing national narratives.** Israeli historiography emphasises the ancient Jewish connection and continuity; Palestinian historiography emphasises the city's many layers and Islamic and Arab centuries, and descent from all the region's peoples; each accuses the other of politicising the past (Wikipedia's historiography section). Khalidi traces modern Palestinian identity's roots to Ottoman-era Jerusalem's elites; Masalha argues for a 4,000-year Palestinian history; Klein and Lemire stress shared life before nationalism hardened. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Khalidi]
- **1967: reunification or occupation.** Israel calls the city its "complete and united" capital and Jerusalem Day marks "reunification"; the UN, EU and most states treat East Jerusalem as occupied, and Palestinians claim it as their capital; Russia recognises West Jerusalem as Israel's and East Jerusalem as Palestine's capital. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Jerusalem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_annexation_of_East_Jerusalem]
- **The status quo on the Mount.** Palestinians and Jordan see growing Jewish prayer and visits as eroding it; Israeli governments insist it is unchanged; a growing number of national-religious rabbis permit visiting, while the Chief Rabbinate's 1967 ruling forbids entry. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Mount]
- **Armstrong's book** was praised for treating all three faiths critically, but a University of Arizona reviewer criticised "puzzling and undocumented assertions" (search snippet). [source: https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/node/4336]

## Conflicts with published chapters
- **Second Temple completion date.** *Ancient Israel and the Levant* says "completed around 516–515 BCE"; Wikipedia's Second Temple article gives "either around 516 BCE or between 450 and 400 BCE". Not a hard contradiction; the writer should say "traditionally dated to about 516 BCE" and not assert a later date.
- **586 vs 587 BCE.** The seed summary says "586 BCE"; *Ancient Israel* says "587 or 586 BCE", which matches the sources. Use "587 or 586 BCE".
- **1099 massacre wording.** *The Crusades* says the crusaders "massacred much of its Muslim and Jewish population"; Wikipedia's History of Jerusalem says "almost all", while modern estimates of 3,000–10,000 dead in a city of perhaps 20,000–30,000 fit "much of". No change needed; avoid "almost all".
- **King David Hotel deaths.** *Partition* "more than 90"; History of Jerusalem "91". Compatible.
- **Ottoman conquest year.** *The Ottomans* gives 1516–1517 for the Mamluk conquest; History of Jerusalem gives 1516 for Jerusalem. Compatible.
- No source contradicts the *Rise of Islam* dates (637–638; Dome 691–692) or *Modern Middle East* statements used here.
- Internal Wikipedia inconsistencies to avoid: walls 1537–1541 vs 1535–1542 and 4,018 m vs 4.5 km (say "about 4 km, built 1537–41"); the pagan temple under the Holy Sepulchre is called Jupiter's in one article and Aphrodite/Venus's in another (say "a pagan temple"); History of Jerusalem says Umar ended "almost three centuries" of Jewish banishment, whereas the ban dated from c. 135 (about five centuries, interrupted briefly in 614–617) — say "ended the ban dating from Hadrian".
