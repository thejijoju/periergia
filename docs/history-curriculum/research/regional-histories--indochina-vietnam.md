## Conquest and the shape of French Indochina
- France concluded the Treaty of Saigon on 5 June 1862, by which the Nguyễn court ceded the provinces of Biên Hòa, Gia Định and Định Tường and the Poulo Condore (Côn Sơn) islands, opened ports and paid an indemnity. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- On 11 August 1863 King Norodom signed a treaty accepting a French protectorate over Cambodia. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Cambodia]
- The Patenôtre Treaty (Treaty of Huế) of 6 June 1884 made Annam and Tonkin French protectorates. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Hu%E1%BA%BF_(1884)]
- The Cần Vương ("Aid the King") movement was a large-scale insurgency of 1885–1896 aiming to restore the boy-emperor Hàm Nghi. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%E1%BA%A7n_V%C6%B0%C6%A1ng] (The *Imperial Expansion* chapter already covers it, including Hàm Nghi's capture in 1888 and exile to Algeria.)
- French Indochina (the Indochinese Union) was formed on 17 October 1887 from Cochinchina, Annam, Tonkin and Cambodia; Laos was added after the Franco-Siamese crisis of 1893 (Wikipedia's lead dates formal inclusion of Laos "from 1899"); Guangzhouwan in China was attached 1898–1945. Capital: Saigon 1887–1902, Hanoi 1902–1945. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- The "three ky": Vietnamese call the three Vietnamese parts Bắc Kỳ (Tonkin), Trung Kỳ (Annam) and Nam Kỳ (Cochinchina). Cochinchina was a directly ruled colony; Annam and Tonkin were protectorates under the Nguyễn emperors, whose decrees had to be approved by the French Resident-Superior or Governor-General. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- The Governor-General held supreme legislative, executive and judicial power in the federation and appointed the residents under him. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In 1893 French gunboats forced the Paknam incident; Siam ceded the lands east of the Mekong; the French protectorate of Laos was built on the Kingdom of Luang Prabang, adding the Principality of Phuan (1899) and Champasak (1904). The French explorer-diplomat Auguste Pavie gave the territory its modern name, Laos. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Laos]
- Siam returned Battambang and Siem Reap provinces to French-ruled Cambodia in 1907. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In 1913 French Indochina had about 16.4 million people (about 14.2 million Vietnamese, 1.6 million Cambodians, 630,000 Laotians), ruled by only around 18,000 French civilians, soldiers and officials. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Laos had an estimated 470,000 people in 1900 and remained a "backwater". France encouraged Vietnamese migration to Laos: by 1943 nearly 40,000 Vietnamese formed the majority in Vientiane (53%), Thakhek (85%) and Pakse (62%), and Vietnamese dominated Laos's colonial civil service. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Laos]
- Indochina was designated a *colonie d'exploitation* (colony of economic exploitation) rather than a settler colony. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]

## Colonial economy: monopolies, corvée, rice, rubber
- Paul Doumer, Governor-General 1897–1902, set out to make the colony pay for itself by taxing opium, wine (alcohol) and salt; Vietnamese, Cambodians and Laotians who could not pay lost houses and land. He also pushed the Indochina–Yunnan railway (approved 1898). (Doumer was later President of France, assassinated in 1932.) [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Doumer]
- The colonial state held a near monopoly on opium, salt and rice alcohol and set consumption quotas for each Vietnamese village, compelling villagers to buy set amounts. These three products supplied about 44% of the colonial budget in 1920, falling to about 20% by 1930. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- The alcohol monopoly banned Vietnamese from distilling their own alcohol; its depots were attacked during the Nghệ-Tĩnh uprising of 1930. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngh%E1%BB%87-T%C4%A9nh_Soviets]
- Public works were built with corvée (forced) labour, and direct and indirect taxes cut peasant living standards. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In Cambodia in 1916 between 30,000 and 100,000 peasants joined a three-month protest against corvée and increased taxes (Milton Osborne's "1916 Affair"); in 1925 the French Resident Félix Bardez was killed by about 700 angry peasants in Kampong Chhnang province (Wikipedia spells it "Kopong Chang") over taxes raised partly to fund the Bokor hill resort. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Cochinchina's Mekong Delta was the colony's rice-export base. By 1930 the French controlled more than a quarter of Cochinchina's farmland; 80% of rice land was owned by 25% of landowners, and 57% of the rural population were landless peasants on large estates. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cochinchina]
- The automobile industry drove a rubber boom; plantations spread especially in Cochinchina and Annam, attracting firms such as Michelin. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Michelin's Phú Riềng plantation (5,500 hectares, Biên Hòa province): official records gave a 1927 death rate of 17% of workers, considered conservative. The rubber workforce "never exceeded 41,000 in any one year" because of deaths and desertions. On 4 February 1930 (some sources: 3 February) the communist-led "Red Phú Riềng" (Phú Riềng Đỏ) strike began. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%C3%BA_Ri%E1%BB%81ng_%C4%90%E1%BB%8F]
- Its leader, Trần Tử Bình (1907–1967), a former Catholic seminarian from a poor peasant family in Hà Nam, had signed up as a plantation labourer in 1927; he was imprisoned on Côn Đảo and later became a general and ambassador to China. His memoir (Vietnamese title *Phú Riềng Đỏ*, 1965) was published in English as *The Red Earth*, edited by David Marr. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_T%E1%BB%AD_B%C3%ACnh ; https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780896801196] Caveat: his sentence is given as 10 years (Wikipedia biography) or 5 years (search excerpt); do not state the length.
- Saigon became the sixth busiest port of the French Empire by 1937; the Trans-Indochinois railway linking Hanoi and Saigon opened in 1936; the Kunming–Haiphong railway linked Tonkin to Yunnan. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Indochina was the second most invested-in French colony by 1940 after Algeria; its bank of issue was the Banque de l'Indochine (founded 1875), which issued the piastre. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Between 1930 and 1932 the French detained more than 12,000 political prisoners, guillotined 88 and sentenced almost 7,000 to prison or hard labour. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cochinchina]
- Poulo Condore (Côn Sơn) prison held thousands of nationalists and communists; concentrated in common cells, prisoners turned it into a "school" for revolution. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]

## First World War
- About 93,000 Indochinese soldiers and workers went to Europe (about 100,000 counting all), mostly from the poorest parts of Annam and Tonkin; 1,150 came from Cambodia. Their costs were charged to the Indochina budget, not to France. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Between 1915 and 1920 Indochina sent 367 million of the 600 million francs France received from its empire; historian Patrice Morlat puts the contribution at 381 million gold francs, about 60% of colonial contributions (excluding Algeria). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Debate: Nguyễn Ái Quốc (Ho Chi Minh) wrote in 1925 that recruits were "taken in chains"; historians such as Joseph Buttinger and Martin Murray accepted forced recruitment, while later work argues most were volunteers or "ostensibly" voluntary. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In 1916 the young Emperor Duy Tân was deposed for backing a rising and exiled with his father to Réunion; Khải Định succeeded him and cooperated closely with France. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Governor-General (later Colonial Minister) Albert Sarraut promised "Franco-Annamite collaboration" while building the Sûreté générale indochinoise, a political police with informants in France, China, Siam and Japan. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]

## Education, quốc ngữ and the "civilising mission"
- The Latin-based Vietnamese alphabet (chữ Quốc ngữ) was developed by Portuguese and other Jesuits from about 1620 (Francisco de Pina); Alexandre de Rhodes's Vietnamese–Portuguese–Latin dictionary was printed in Rome in 1651. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_alphabet]
- The French administration enforced quốc ngữ from 1910; since the 1920s Vietnamese have mostly written in it; the Confucian examination system was suppressed (Wikipedia gives 1917) forcing elites into French-language schooling. Critics (e.g. Pears) argue it cut Vietnamese off from Hán-Nôm literature; nationalists and reformers also embraced it to democratise learning. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_alphabet]
- On 16 May 1906 Governor-General Paul Beau created Councils for the Improvement of Indigenous Education. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- The Tonkin Free School (Đông Kinh Nghĩa Thục), founded in Hanoi in March 1907 by Lương Văn Can and others linked to Phan Châu Trinh and Phan Bội Châu, taught modern subjects and promoted quốc ngữ; the French closed it in November 1907. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkin_Free_School]
- Under French rule French became the language of the elite and administration, especially in Tonkin and Cochinchina. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]

## Early anticolonialism: Phan Bội Châu and Phan Châu Trinh
- Phan Bội Châu (26 December 1867 – 29 October 1940), from Nghệ An, founded the Duy Tân Hội (Modernisation Association) in 1904 with Prince Cường Để and launched the Đông Du ("Journey to the East") movement sending students to Japan. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_B%E1%BB%99i_Ch%C3%A2u]
- His *Việt Nam vong quốc sử* (History of the Loss of Vietnam, 1905), published with Liang Qichao's help, helped revive the name "Vietnam". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_B%E1%BB%99i_Ch%C3%A2u]
- Students in Japan peaked at about 200 in 1908 (Đông Du article: over 100 by October 1907); under French pressure Japan expelled them in 1908–09. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_B%E1%BB%99i_Ch%C3%A2u ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%C3%B4ng_Du]
- In 1912, inspired by China's 1911 revolution, Phan Bội Châu replaced the Duy Tân Hội with the republican Việt Nam Quang Phục Hội (Vietnamese Restoration League). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_B%E1%BB%99i_Ch%C3%A2u]
- French agents seized Phan Bội Châu in Shanghai in 1925; convicted of treason, he was released on 24 December 1925 by Governor-General Varenne after public protest and lived under house arrest in Huế until his death. One account (cited in Wikipedia, "allegedly") says Ho Chi Minh, then in Canton, tipped off the French for money. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_B%E1%BB%99i_Ch%C3%A2u ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Phan Châu Trinh (9 September 1872 – 24 March 1926) rejected violence and foreign help, seeking to use French democratic principles; his 1906 letter to Governor-General Beau asked France to live up to its civilising mission. Slogans: "Broaden the people's minds, invigorate the people's spirit, improve the people's livelihood." [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_Ch%C3%A2u_Trinh]
- After the 1908 tax revolts in Annam he was sentenced to death, commuted, and sent to Côn Đảo; pardoned in 1911, he went to France, where he worked with the Group of Vietnamese Patriots (including the young Nguyễn Tất Thành) at 6 Villa des Gobelins. Over 60,000 attended his funeral in 1926, sparking protests. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_Ch%C3%A2u_Trinh]
- In June 1908 the "Hanoi Poison Plot" by Vietnamese colonial soldiers tried to poison the Hanoi garrison to help Đề Thám's rebels; the French blamed both Phans. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In Laos, the Holy Man's Rebellion of upland Lao Theung peoples (1901–1907) and resistance on the Bolaven Plateau lasting to 1936 challenged French rule. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Historian David Marr argues the 1920s marked a shift from "traditional" to "modern" national consciousness, with returnees from France "urbanising" and "politicising" Vietnamese nationalism. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]

## The VNQDĐ and Yên Bái (1930)
- The Việt Nam Quốc Dân Đảng (VNQDĐ, Vietnamese Nationalist Party), modelled broadly on the Chinese Kuomintang, was founded in Hanoi on 25 December 1927, led by Nguyễn Thái Học (1 December 1902 – 17 June 1930). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Nationalist_Party ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Th%C3%A1i_H%E1%BB%8Dc]
- The assassination on 9 February 1929 of Bazin, a hated French labour recruiter for the plantations, triggered a crackdown; 78 men were jailed for 5–20 years. (His first name is given inconsistently, Hervé or Alfred; use the surname only.) [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Nationalist_Party]
- On the night of 10 February 1930 about 50 Vietnamese soldiers of the 4th Tonkinese Rifles at Yên Bái, with about 60 VNQDĐ civilians, turned on their French officers; the mutiny failed within 24 hours as most soldiers stayed loyal. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C3%AAn_B%C3%A1i_mutiny]
- On 16 February French aircraft bombed the VNQDĐ base village of Cổ Am, killing about 200, mostly civilians. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Th%C3%A1i_H%E1%BB%8Dc]
- Nguyễn Thái Học was among 39 sentenced to death and one of 13 guillotined at Yên Bái on 17 June 1930; the condemned cried "Viet Nam!". Nguyễn Thị Giang, a VNQDĐ member and his fiancée, is listed among the dead. The party never regained its strength; remnants fled to Yunnan. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Th%C3%A1i_H%E1%BB%8Dc ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C3%AAn_B%C3%A1i_mutiny]

## Ho Chi Minh, the ICP and the Nghệ-Tĩnh soviets
- Ho Chi Minh was born Nguyễn Sinh Cung on 19 May 1890 in Nghệ An (he also used 1891, 1892, 1894, 1895) and died 2 September 1969; he used perhaps 50–200 aliases, including Nguyễn Tất Thành and Nguyễn Ái Quốc ("Nguyễn the Patriot"). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- He left Saigon on 5 June 1911 as a kitchen helper on the *Amiral de Latouche-Tréville*, reaching Marseille on 5 July 1911, then worked his way around the world. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- In 1919 the Group of Vietnamese Patriots petitioned the Versailles peace conference (letters to Clemenceau and Wilson) for Vietnamese rights; they were ignored. The idea of a lost "Wilsonian moment" is disputed, since he was already committed to socialism. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- In December 1920 at the Congress of Tours he voted for the Third International and became a founding member of the French Communist Party. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- In 1923 he went to Moscow to work for the Comintern; in 1924–25 he was sent to Canton, where he founded the Vietnamese Revolutionary Youth League (1925). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- The Vietnamese Communist Party was founded on 3 February 1930 in Hong Kong by merging rival communist groups, with Ho chairing the meeting; on Comintern instructions it was renamed the Indochinese Communist Party (ICP) in October 1930 to cover Cambodia and Laos too. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochinese_Communist_Party ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- The ICP "dissolved" itself on 11 November 1945 but continued clandestinely; it reappeared as the Workers' Party of Vietnam in February 1951 (Communist Party of Vietnam from 1976); separate Cambodian and Lao parties emerged from it. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochinese_Communist_Party]
- In June 1931 Ho was arrested in Hong Kong; his solicitor Frank Loseby fought extradition to the Privy Council, and he was reported dead in 1932. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- The Nghệ-Tĩnh soviets (1930–31): strikes in Vinh and Bến Thủy from March 1930 spread to peasants demanding an end to head taxes and corvée; on 12 September 1930 French planes bombed demonstrators at Hưng Nguyên, killing 140–200. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngh%E1%BB%87-T%C4%A9nh_Soviets]
- Repression: by early 1932 tens of thousands were imprisoned and 99% of ICP cadres killed or arrested; Bernal estimates over 1,300 killed and about 1,000 more dying in prisons and camps; fewer than 200 on the colonial side died, one of them French. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngh%E1%BB%87-T%C4%A9nh_Soviets]
- Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai (1910–1941), from Vinh, was a leading ICP organiser, worked for Ho in the Comintern's Hong Kong office (1930), attended the 7th Comintern Congress in 1935, and was executed by French firing squad in 1941. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Th%E1%BB%8B_Minh_Khai]
- The Trotskyist Tạ Thu Thâu (1906–1945) and the paper *La Lutte* allied with ICP communists in Saigon municipal elections in 1937 and beat both communists and Constitutionalists in the April 1939 Cochinchina Council elections; the Viet Minh executed him in September 1945. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E1%BA%A1_Thu_Th%C3%A2u ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- The 1936–38 Popular Front briefly amnestied prisoners and eased censorship under Governor-General Brévié. In November 1940 the ICP's rising in Cochinchina (Nam Kỳ) was crushed. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- New religious movements in the south: Cao Đài, founded in Tây Ninh in 1926 (declared 7 October 1926), and Hòa Hảo, founded in 1939 by Huỳnh Phú Sổ; both later fielded armies. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_%C4%90%C3%A0i ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B2a_H%E1%BA%A3o]
- Cambodian nationalism: Son Ngoc Thanh and Pach Chhoeun founded *Nagaravatta*, the first Khmer-language newspaper, in 1936; the arrest of the monk Hem Chieu in July 1942 led to the "Umbrella" demonstration. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Ngoc_Thanh ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Cambodia]

## Second World War, the Japanese coup and the famine
- In September 1940 Vichy France granted Japan military access to Tonkin; Japanese troops controlled the whole colony by 1941 while the French administration under Admiral Jean Decoux remained; there were 140,000 Japanese troops by 1941. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_famine_of_1945]
- Thailand fought the Franco-Thai War (October 1940 – 9 May 1941) and gained territory under Japanese mediation. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In May 1941, at Pác Bó, Ho Chi Minh founded the Việt Minh (League for the Independence of Vietnam) at the ICP's Eighth Plenum. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution]
- Japan's coup of 9 March 1945 (Operation Meigō/"Bright Moon") overthrew the French administration; at Lạng Sơn 544 Europeans were killed (387 of them executed after capture) and 1,832 Tonkinese colonial troops. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat_in_French_Indochina]
- Under Japanese direction Bảo Đại signed a declaration of Vietnamese independence on 11 March 1945 (Empire of Vietnam; Trần Trọng Kim cabinet formed 17 April 1945); Sihanouk proclaimed the Kingdom of Kampuchea on 12 or 13 March 1945 (sources differ); Luang Prabang declared independence on 8 April 1945. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%A3o_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Vietnam ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kampuchea_(1945) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Ngoc_Thanh ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_Issara]
- The famine of 1944–45 (Nạn đói Ất Dậu), from October 1944 to late 1945 in northern Vietnam: estimates range from 400,000 to 2 million dead. In May 1945 twenty provinces reported 380,000 starved and 20,000 dead of disease; a French military report of October 1945 estimated half a million; Decoux's memoirs said about a million; Ho Chi Minh's 2 September declaration gave 2 million (some read "over 2 million"), still the Party's official figure. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_famine_of_1945 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Imperial Commissioner Phan Kế Toại put the toll at 400,000; Furuta Motoo, after joint Vietnamese–Japanese fieldwork in the early 1990s (report edited by Văn Tạo and Furuta), judged it probably over 1 million but was sceptical of 2 million; David Marr gives 1 million or more in Tonkin and northern Annam; Gregg Huff (*Economic History Review*, 2019) works with about a million deaths. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina ; search excerpt of Marr chapter via Cambridge Core: https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A614B3A305F48E97202B1AC844155B15/9781107105089c7_106-124.pdf/the-august-revolution-of-1945.pdf ; https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:ehsrev:v:72:y:2019:i:1:p:286-316]
- Causes: French compulsory rice purchases at a fixed 1.40 piastres per 10 kg while market prices rose to 6–7 piastres by June 1944; Japanese and French requisitions; forced planting of jute and other industrial crops; rice and maize burned as fuel in power stations after US bombing cut coal supplies; bombing of transport and dikes; and drought and the floods of August–September 1944. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_famine_of_1945]
- The Việt Minh grew from guerrilla band to mass movement by leading seizures of Japanese rice granaries. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_famine_of_1945]
- Americans and the Việt Minh: OSS officer Archimedes Patti met Ho in late April 1945; the OSS "Deer Team" parachuted in from 16 July 1945, trained about 40 Việt Minh recruits, and an OSS medic treated Ho. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]

## The August Revolution and 2 September 1945
- After Japan's surrender on 15 August 1945 the Việt Minh launched its "General Uprising" (16–30 August); its National Congress met at Tân Trào on 16 August, and it took Hanoi on 19 August. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution]
- Bảo Đại abdicated: Wikipedia articles give 25 August (announcement) and the public ceremony at the Meridian Gate in Huế on 30 August 1945, where he handed the seal and sword to Trần Huy Liệu and became "citizen Vĩnh Thụy"; he then became "supreme advisor" to the new government. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%A3o_%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution]
- On 2 September 1945 at Ba Đình Square, Hanoi, Ho read the Declaration of Independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, opening with the American Declaration of Independence and referring to the French Declaration of the Rights of Man. No foreign state recognised the DRV for five years. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_independence_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_Vietnam]
- Pierre Asselin reads the American references as a strategic bid for Allied (US) support that concealed Ho's communist ties. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_independence_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_Vietnam]
- North of the 16th parallel about 200,000 Chinese Nationalist troops under Lu Han took the Japanese surrender (staying until mid-1946); British forces under Mountbatten's command occupied the south. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Civil conflict among Vietnamese: the Việt Minh repressed Trotskyists and non-communist nationalists; in 1946 Ho's subordinates imprisoned 2,500 non-communist nationalists and forced 6,000 to flee, and hundreds of VNQDĐ and Đại Việt members were executed or exiled in July 1946. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh]
- The Ho–Sainteny agreement of 6 March 1946 recognised the DRV as a "free state" within the Indochinese Federation and French Union and let French troops return north of the 16th parallel; the Fontainebleau talks that summer failed. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%E2%80%93Sainteny_agreement ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- In Laos, Prince Phetsarath's Lao Issara ("Free Laos") proclaimed independence on 12 October 1945; it dissolved on 14 October 1949. In Cambodia the French arrested Son Ngoc Thanh on 12 October 1945 and restored control. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_Issara ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kampuchea_(1945)]

## First Indochina War, 1946–1954
- The war ran from 19 December 1946 (fighting in Hanoi) to 11 August 1954. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War]
- The French bombardment of Haiphong on 23 November 1946 killed between 2,000 and 6,000 Vietnamese (6,000 per Paul Mus, the most cited figure; Ho's own letter said about 3,000; General Valluy 300; Việt Minh propaganda 10,000–20,000). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiphong_incident]
- France built the "Bảo Đại solution": the Élysée Accords of 1949 created the State of Vietnam within the French Union, brought into effect by Bảo Đại on 14 June 1949. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Vietnam ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- 1950 was the turning point: China and the USSR recognised the DRV; in October 1950 the French lost about 6,000 men on Route Coloniale 4 (Cao Bằng). From 1950 to 1956 China shipped 155,000 small arms, 4,630 artillery pieces and much else. By 1954 the US paid about 80% of France's war costs. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War]
- French Union forces included Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian and West African colonial troops, Foreign Legionnaires and Vietnamese, Lao and Cambodian soldiers; metropolitan conscripts were not sent; the French left called it *la sale guerre* ("the dirty war"). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- French atrocities included the Mỹ Trạch massacre of 29 November 1947 (over 200 women and children killed). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War]
- Cambodia (Sihanouk's "Royal Crusade") proclaimed full independence on 9 November 1953; Laos became independent in October 1953; the State of Vietnam on 4 June 1954 (still within the French Union). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Sihanouk ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Pathet Lao: in August 1950 Prince Souphanouvong ("the Red Prince") convened the Lao Freedom Front (Neo Lao Issara) at Việt Minh headquarters in Tuyên Quang; in April 1953 about 40,000 Việt Minh troops with some 2,000 Pathet Lao invaded northeastern Laos. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souphanouvong ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War]
- Dien Bien Phu, 13 March – 7 May 1954, under Võ Nguyên Giáp (French: 1,142–2,293 killed, 11,721 captured): see the published *Vietnam War* and *Decolonization* chapters. Viet Minh losses are put at about 25,000 casualties with as many as 10,000 killed (First Indochina War article). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dien_Bien_Phu ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War]
- War dead (estimates): Việt Minh 175,000–300,000 dead or missing (Western historians) or 191,605 (Vietnamese government); French Union 74,220 dead (20,685 from metropolitan France); State of Vietnam 58,877 dead or missing; total 400,000–842,707 killed, including 125,000–400,000 civilians. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War]

## Geneva 1954 and the two Vietnams
- The Geneva Conference ran 26 April – 21 July 1954; Indochina talks began 8 May, the day after Dien Bien Phu fell. Three ceasefire agreements covered Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia. Vietnam was divided at the 17th parallel, the Pathet Lao were confined to two northern provinces, and Khmer Issarak forces disbanded. The Final Declaration, not legally binding, called for elections in July 1956. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Geneva_Conference]
- Zhou Enlai pressed the Viet Minh to withdraw from Laos and Cambodia and accept partition, a blow to the DRV's hopes of leading Lao and Khmer allies. Pierre Mendès France, prime minister from June 1954, promised a ceasefire within 30 days. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Geneva_Conference]
- The US and the State of Vietnam did not sign; Diệm rejected the elections, arguing free voting was impossible in the North. French troops left the South on 28 April 1956. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- Nearly one million northerners moved South and about 150,000 southerners went North. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_North_Vietnam]
- Northern land reform (1953–56), modelled on China's with Chinese advisers, was pushed by Trường Chinh, who was dismissed as General Secretary after its failure in 1956. A Politburo document of 4 May 1953 fixed executions "in principle" at one per thousand people (about 15,000). The first landlord executed (9 July 1953), Nguyễn Thị Năm, had supported the communist resistance. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_North_Vietnam ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C6%B0%E1%BB%9Dng_Chinh]
- Execution estimates range from 800 to 200,000: Edwin Moïse 3,000–15,000, later about 13,500; economist Vo Nhan Tri cited a party document of 15,000 wrongful executions; Tuong Vu (2007) finds the number lower than reported at the time but probably above 13,500 including suicides; 50,000 was once widely accepted. Of 172,008 people classified as landlords or rich peasants, 71.66% were wrongly categorised (Vietnam Institute for Economics). A "Rectification of Errors" campaign followed (1956–57). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_North_Vietnam]
- Ngô Đình Diệm (3 January 1901 – 2 November 1963), a Catholic from a mandarin family of Huế, built an ideology from Mounier's Personalism and Confucianism (the Cần Lao Party); for his South, the Buddhist crisis and his overthrow, see the *Vietnam War* chapter. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngo_Dinh_Diem]

## Cambodia and Laos, 1953–1975
- Norodom Sihanouk (31 October 1922 – 15 October 2012) was chosen king by Governor-General Decoux in 1941 (crowned 3 May 1941); abdicated on 2 March 1955 in favour of his father to enter politics; his Sangkum won the 1955 elections; he became Chief of State in 1960. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Sihanouk]
- Sihanouk coined *Khmers rouges* for his leftist opponents. The future Khmer Rouge leaders (Saloth Sar/Pol Pot, Ieng Sary, Khieu Samphan) studied in Paris, where Pol Pot and Ieng Sary joined the French Communist Party around 1949–51. The 1967 Samlaut uprising in Battambang preceded their insurgency (from 1968). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Sihanouk]
- Lon Nol's coup of March 1970 deposed Sihanouk, who then allied with the Khmer Rouge. US bombing and Cambodian civil war: Ben Kiernan attributes 50,000–150,000 deaths to US bombing; Heuveline puts civil-war deaths at "300,000 or less". [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide]
- Laos: Kaysone Phomvihane (1920–1992), son of a Vietnamese colonial civil servant and a Lao mother, founded the Lao Issara army on 20 January 1949, joined the ICP that July, and became general secretary of the Lao People's Party founded 22 March 1955 (later Lao People's Revolutionary Party). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaysone_Phomvihane]
- The CIA's "Secret War" used about 30,000 Hmong, Mien and Khmu irregulars under General Vang Pao; see *Vietnam War* chapter for the bombing of Laos. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War]

## 1975 and after
- Saigon fell on 30 April 1975 (see *Vietnam War* chapter); the Khmer Rouge took Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975 and emptied the cities; the country was renamed Democratic Kampuchea in 1976. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge]
- Laos: the Lao monarchy ended when King Savang Vatthana abdicated on 2 December 1975 and the Lao People's Democratic Republic was proclaimed; Kaysone became prime minister (1975–91), Souphanouvong president (1975–86). The king, queen and crown prince died in labour camps (around 1981 reported). Thousands were sent to "re-education" for up to ten years; about a third of Lao Hmong left the country; "up to 300,000" fled to Thailand overall (weakly sourced). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Laos ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaysone_Phomvihane ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souphanouvong]
- Vietnam was formally reunified as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on 2 July 1976. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_independence_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_Vietnam]
- Re-education camps: ARVN soldiers were told to bring supplies for three days, ten days or a month; many stayed years, some up to 18 years (higher officers often over 10). At least 200,000–300,000 were imprisoned; other estimates say 500,000 to 1 million passed through. A June 1976 promise of trial or release after three years was not honoured. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-education_camp_(Vietnam)]
- Boat people: the large exodus began in September 1978; in May 1978 the ethnic Chinese (Hoa) began leaving in large numbers, and by end-1979 250,000 Hoa had fled to China; arrivals peaked at 54,000 in June 1979; a UN conference in Geneva in July 1979 created orderly departures and faster resettlement. Almost 800,000 reached other countries 1975–1995; UNHCR estimates 200,000–250,000 died at sea (other figures in the published chapter: up to 400,000; Barry Wain 30,000). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_boat_people]
- Khmer Rouge deaths: 1.5 to 2 million (20–25% of a 1975 population of about 7.8 million); Kiernan 1.671–1.871 million; Sliwinski just under 2 million; Heuveline 1.17–3.42 million excess deaths 1970–79 (later 1.2–2.8 million under the Khmer Rouge); the PRK's 3.3 million is considered exaggerated. DC-Cam has mapped 23,745 mass graves with about 1.3 million suspected execution victims. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide]
- About 20,000 people passed through Security Prison 21 (Tuol Sleng), one of 196 prisons; only seven adults survived (Wikipedia's figure). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide]
- Victims included 200,000–300,000 Chinese Cambodians, 90,000–500,000 Cham (Kiernan's 1982 90,000; DC-Cam/UN 500,000–560,000) and about 20,000 Vietnamese. Radio Phnom Penh called on Cambodians to "exterminate the 50 million Vietnamese". The Khmer Rouge Tribunal convicted Duch (Kang Kek Iew) in 2010 and, in November 2018, Nuon Chea and Khieu Samphan of genocide against the Vietnamese (Nuon Chea also against the Cham). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge]

## The Third Indochina War: Cambodia 1978–89 and China 1979
- Khmer Rouge cross-border raids killed some 30,000 Vietnamese civilians 1975–78 (Vietnamese government figure), including the Ba Chúc massacre of April 1978 (3,157 killed). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War]
- Vietnam signed a Treaty of Friendship and Cooperation with the USSR on 3 November 1978; it sponsored the Kampuchean United Front for National Salvation under Heng Samrin, a Khmer Rouge defector. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War]
- On 25 December 1978 about 150,000 Vietnamese troops (13 divisions) invaded; they entered Phnom Penh on 7 January 1979; the People's Republic of Kampuchea was established on 8 January 1979, beginning a ten-year occupation. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War]
- The UN kept recognising Democratic Kampuchea (vote of September 1979); the resistance formed the Coalition Government of Democratic Kampuchea in 1982; Hun Sen (evacuated to Vietnam with withdrawing Vietnamese forces in early 1978) led the PRK government as prime minister; Vietnam withdrew in September 1989. Vietnamese losses: about 15,000 killed (infobox figure). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge]
- Sino-Vietnamese War: Deng Xiaoping told Carter that China would teach Vietnam "a lesson"; on 17 February 1979 about 200,000 Chinese troops invaded; Lạng Sơn fell on 6 March, when China declared its punitive mission achieved; Chinese troops left by 16 March. Relations were normalised only in 1991. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_War]
- Casualties are disputed: China admits 6,954 killed (Vietnam claims 62,000 Chinese casualties incl. 26,000 dead); Vietnam admits about 7,000 soldiers killed, 4,000 missing and 10,000 civilians killed (China claims 42,000–57,000 soldiers and 70,000 militia); a Western estimate is about 30,000 Vietnamese killed. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_War]

## Đổi Mới
- Before reform inflation exceeded 700% and Soviet aid fell; Lê Duẩn (General Secretary, died 10 July 1986) was succeeded after the 6th Party Congress (15–18 December 1986) by the reformer Nguyễn Văn Linh, former NLF leader; Võ Văn Kiệt delivered the economic report. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%95i_M%E1%BB%9Bi ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%AA_Du%E1%BA%A9n]
- Đổi Mới ("renovation") aimed at a "socialist-oriented market economy"; partial farm reforms had begun in August 1979; the 1988 Land Law recognised private land-use rights and Resolution 10 freed farmers from compulsory cooperatives and let them sell on the market. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%95i_M%E1%BB%9Bi]

## Debates
- **Nationalism vs communism.** Was the revolution essentially nationalist with a communist label, or driven by communist ideology? Ho's own 1960 account puts patriotism first. David Marr traced a modern nationalism emerging in the 1920s–30s and sought a less triumphalist, more contingent account of 1945 than the Party's. Tuong Vu (2017) argues ideology was central, challenging the image of a revolution "driven solely by patriotic inspirations", though reviewers note he also sees nationalism and Marxism-Leninism as intertwined. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina ; https://usvietnamcenter.uoregon.edu/vietnams-communist-revolution-the-power-and-limits-of/ ; https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/AF60F852C6F18CE5E24195BE1049E102/S0022463416000527a.pdf/david-marrs-vietnamese-revolution.pdf]
- **One Vietnam or many?** Goscha stresses contingency and rival non-communist republican visions; his *Going Indochinese* (2012) shows Vietnamese nationalists imagining their future in Indochinese terms. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Goscha ; search summary of Goscha's book]
- **The war as a Vietnamese civil war.** Violence among Vietnamese from 1945 (Việt Minh vs VNQDĐ, Đại Việt, Trotskyists, Cao Đài, Hòa Hảo) supports reading the wars as civil as well as anticolonial; Keith Taylor argues earlier Western scholarship recycled Hanoi's propaganda; Lien-Hang Nguyen shows Lê Duẩn and Lê Đức Thọ in control of Hanoi's strategy from about 1960. The orthodox vs revisionist debate on the American war (Moyar etc.) is laid out in the published *Vietnam War* chapter; cross-refer. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution ; https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2013/10/new-book-explores-history-vietnamese ; https://www.lrb.co.uk/v35/n03/andrew-bacevich/small-nations-take-heed]
- **"Ambiguous colonization".** Brocheux and Hémery's framing of French rule as both exploitative and modernising (rubber, railways, schools, quốc ngữ) versus nationalist and Marxist accounts of pure exploitation (e.g. Ngô Vĩnh Long). [source: https://muse.jhu.edu/article/458302/summary]
- **Who caused the famine?** Bui Minh Dung blames the Japanese occupation as the direct cause; others stress French rice requisitions, bombing and natural disasters; Hanoi blamed both France and Japan; the death toll itself (400,000 to 2 million) is political. Huff (2019) finds differences in endowments and entitlements explain who died. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_famine_of_1945 ; https://ehs.org.uk/article/causes-and-consequences-of-the-great-vietnam-famine-1944-5]
- **First World War recruitment:** forced (Ho, Buttinger, Murray) vs largely voluntary (later historians). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina]
- **Cambodia:** whether the Khmer Rouge killings were genocide (Kiernan; the 2018 tribunal verdict) or not racially motivated (Vickery, Heder — see *Modern Southeast Asia*); how far US bombing (Kiernan, Etcheson) explains the Khmer Rouge rise. Vietnam's 1979 invasion is remembered in Vietnam and by many Cambodians as liberation but was treated internationally as aggression (UN seat kept by Democratic Kampuchea). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War]
- **Land reform death toll** (800 to 200,000; Moïse ~13,500; Vu Tuong) — see figures above. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_North_Vietnam]

## Conflicts with published chapters
- *Vietnam War*: says "In 1930 Nguyen Sinh Cung … founded the Indochinese Communist Party". Sources: he chaired the Hong Kong meeting that founded the *Vietnamese* Communist Party on 3 February 1930, renamed ICP in October 1930 on Comintern orders. Not a contradiction, but this chapter should be precise.
- *Vietnam War*: land-reform executions "from under 2,000 to about 50,000". Wikipedia now gives a range of 800 to 200,000 (with ~100,000 extrapolated from witness testimony). Moïse's 13,500 is consistent.
- *Vietnam War*: re-education "at least 200,000–300,000 … some estimates as high as 500,000". Wikipedia now gives "500,000 to 1 million" as the upper estimates.
- *Vietnam War*: declaration quoted as "All men are created equal. They are endowed by their Creator…"; the version I could verify (Karnow, via Wikipedia) reads "We hold the truth that all men are created equal, that they are endowed…". Translation difference, not a factual conflict; keep consistent wording across chapters or note it.
- *Vietnam War* / *Decolonization*: Bảo Đại's abdication is dated "August 1945"; sources give 25 August (announcement) and 30 August (ceremony in Huế). Not a conflict; be precise.
- *Decolonization*: "The Viet Minh took about 11,000 prisoners; only 3,290 came back" vs Dien Bien Phu infobox 11,721 captured. Consistent if "about 11,000" is read loosely.
- *Modern Southeast Asia*: dates the tribunal's genocide verdict to 2018 (Khmer Rouge article: November 2018), but the Cambodian genocide article's text says "28 March 2019". The 2018 date appears correct; flag only.
- *Modern Southeast Asia*: Laos added to Indochina "after the crisis of 1893"; Wikipedia's lead says Laos part of the Union "from 1899". Both can be stated: protectorate 1893, formally integrated 1899.
- Famine: all published chapters say 400,000–2 million, consistent with sources. Note the *French Indochina* article elsewhere says "one to two million" and "200 thousand to 2 million"; keep 400,000–2 million.
- No published chapter conflicts found on Dien Bien Phu dates, Geneva, Khmer Rouge death tolls (1.5–2 million), boat-people figures, or Đổi Mới (1986, inflation over 700%).
