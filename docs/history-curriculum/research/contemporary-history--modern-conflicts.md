## 1. The Gulf War (1990–91) and the "new world order"
- Iraq invaded Kuwait on 2 August 1990. The UN Security Council demanded withdrawal the same day (Res. 660) and imposed a total embargo four days later (Res. 661, 6 August). [GW]
- Res. 678 (29 November 1990) authorised member states to use "all necessary means" if Iraq had not left Kuwait by 15 January 1991. [GW]
- A US-led coalition of 42 countries ("the broadest military alliance since World War II"); largest contributors the US, Saudi Arabia, the UK and Egypt. Operation Desert Shield (build-up, Aug 1990–Jan 1991); Operation Desert Storm: air campaign from 17 January 1991, ground offensive 24 February, ceasefire declared within 100 hours; Kuwait liberated 28 February. Coalition strength over 950,000; Iraq 1,000,000+ (~600,000 in Kuwait). [GW]
- Iraq fired missiles at Israel and Saudi Arabia (74 killed in Israel per GW infobox); a Scud on a US barracks in Dhahran on 25 February killed 28 US soldiers. [GW]
- Retreating Iraqi troops set fire to Kuwait's oil wells (GW gives 737 wells in one passage, "nearly 700"/"700" elsewhere — say "some 700"); fires burned about 10 months, the last extinguished in November 1991. The retreating convoy bombed on the main Iraq–Kuwait highway, 26–27 February, became known as the "Highway of Death". [GW]
- Casualties (GW infobox, ranged): coalition 292 killed (147 by enemy action); Kuwait 420 military killed, over 1,000 Kuwaiti civilians killed and 600 missing; Iraqi military 20,000–50,000 killed (Bourque); Iraqi civilians 3,664 killed directly. Medact's total for Iraqi losses including the post-war uprisings: 142,500–206,000 (100–120,000 military, 3–15,000 civilians in the war, 35–65,000 civilians in the uprisings). Present as estimates that vary widely. [GW]
- **"New world order":** Bush's address to a joint session of Congress, 11 September 1990 ("Toward a New World Order"). NWO notes Gorbachev had used the phrase first (nuclear disarmament, a stronger UN, great-power co-operation) and that after Bush's speech his idealism was compared to Wilson and FDR. [NWO; WebSearch: https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-speeches/september-11-1990-address-joint-session-congress]
- **Kurds and Shia after 1991:** Bush on 15 February 1991 (via Voice of America) urged "the Iraqi military and the Iraqi people to take matters into their own hands"; a CIA-linked "Voice of Free Iraq" radio called for revolt on 24 February. Shia and Kurdish uprisings, March–April 1991, took most cities in two weeks but were crushed by the Republican Guard; the US did not intervene in the south, "leaving a legacy of mistrust among the Shia Arabs". Deaths 25,000–180,000, mostly civilians (IU infobox — very wide). Mass graves found after 2003; of 200 registered by the Iraqi Human Rights Ministry 2003–06 most were in the south, one thought to hold up to 10,000. [IU]
- Nearly two million Iraqis fled in March–early April 1991, 1.5 million of them Kurds; by 6 April UNHCR estimated ~750,000 Iraqi Kurds had fled to Iran and 280,000 to Turkey, with 300,000 more at the Turkish border; Iran opened its border, Turkey at first closed it. [IU]
- UNSC Res. 688 (5 April 1991) condemned repression of the Kurds. Operation Provide Comfort (from April 1991; UK name Operation Haven, pressed by John Major) protected and fed Kurdish refugees; the northern no-fly zone made possible the semi-autonomous Kurdistan Region; a southern no-fly zone (south of the 32nd parallel) followed in August 1992. The Kurdish parties then fought a civil war until a US-brokered ceasefire in September 1998. Saddam intensified draining of the Mesopotamian marshes and relocation of Marsh Arabs. [IU; OPC]
- Somalia: the UN intervened to relieve the 1992 famine; in the Battle of Mogadishu (3–4 October 1993; "Black Hawk Down") 18 US soldiers died; Somali dead, insurgents and civilians, most estimates 133–700. It ended Operation Gothic Serpent and UNOSOM II military operations — and made Washington wary of intervening in Rwanda six months later (writer: the last clause is an inference; MOG supports only the first two). [MOG]

## 2. Yugoslavia's wars and Srebrenica (brief; see soviet-collapse notes §15)
- Yugoslav Wars, 1991–2001 (Ten-Day War in Slovenia 27 Jun–7 Jul 1991; Croatia 1991–95; Bosnia 6 Apr 1992–14 Dec 1995; Kosovo 1998–99; Preševo Valley and Macedonia insurgencies to 2001). Deaths: International Center for Transitional Justice 140,000; Humanitarian Law Center at least 130,000; civilians 52–56%; c. 4 million displaced. [YW]
- Bosnia: RDC ("Bosnian Book of the Dead") June 2012: 101,040 dead or disappeared — 62,013 Bosniaks (61.4%), 24,953 Serbs (24.7%), 8,403 Croats (8.3%); its 2007 list of 97,207 names is "considered the most authoritative account" and "believed to be a slight undercount". [BOS]
- **Srebrenica, July 1995:** more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys killed by the Army of Republika Srpska under Ratko Mladić (Scorpions paramilitaries also took part); 25,000–30,000 Bosniaks, mainly women and children, forcibly removed. The UN had declared Srebrenica a "safe area"; 370 lightly armed Dutch UN troops did not deter its capture; on 13 July peacekeepers handed over some 5,000 Muslims sheltering at their base in exchange for 14 Dutch hostages. List of missing/killed: 8,372 names; RDC: 83% of those killed were civilians; nearly 7,000 identified by DNA as of 2020. First legally recognised genocide in Europe since WWII — ICTY Appeals Chamber (2004), ICJ (2007). Dutch government resigned 2002; Dutch courts (2013, 2014, 2019) found the state liable for failing to prevent more than 300 deaths. Serbian president Nikolić apologised in 2013 for "the crime" but refused to call it genocide. UN day of commemoration on 11 July designated May 2024. [SRE]
- Tribunals: Karadžić convicted of genocide at Srebrenica in 2016, ultimately sentenced to life; Mladić convicted 2017 on 10 counts including genocide, life imprisonment. [SRE; BOS]
- Kosovo: an estimated 800,000 Kosovo Albanians fled and 7,000–9,000 were killed by Yugoslav forces (NYT, as cited in KOS; US State Dept used 10,000). NATO bombed from 24 March 1999 without UNSC authorisation; the Independent International Commission on Kosovo concluded "the NATO military intervention was illegal but legitimate". Kosovo declared independence 17 February 2008. [KOS]

## 3. Rwanda, 1994, and the Great Lakes wars
- Genocide 7 April–19 July 1994 (≈100 days), during the Rwandan Civil War (begun 1990 when the RPF, a predominantly Tutsi rebel army, invaded from Uganda; Arusha Accords 1993). Triggered by the shooting down of President Habyarimana's plane on 6 April 1994. Tutsi, and moderate Hutu and Twa, killed by the army, Presidential Guard, gendarmerie and *interahamwe* militias "aided by local populations"; victims "often murdered by neighbours". [RG]
- Death toll — see consistency note (a): most scholarly estimates 500,000–662,000 Tutsi; HRW 507,000; JGR 2020 symposium 500,000–600,000 (≈ two-thirds of Rwanda's Tutsi); Rwandan government 1,074,017. Rape: 250,000–500,000 cases. About 400,000 orphans; 30% of Batwa killed; ~2 million Rwandans, mostly Hutu, fled. [RG]
- Mechanics: roadblocks checked national identity cards that recorded ethnicity; anyone with a Tutsi card was killed. Hate radio RTLM (Radio Télévision Libre des Mille Collines) was founded by Hutu Power hardliners and reached rural audiences that newspapers did not. [RG]
- UN: UNAMIR (from October 1993) under Roméo Dallaire had a Chapter VI mandate; his request to raid arms caches was refused by UN DPKO; UNAMIR sheltered thousands at Amahoro Stadium; Dallaire later called it a "failure". On 9 April, 1,000 European troops arrived to evacuate Europeans and did not stay. French-led Opération Turquoise from 23 June (~2,500 troops) "not able to save significant numbers of lives"; génocidaires displayed French flags. The RPF took Kigali 4 July (now Liberation Day); génocidaires and masses of Hutu fled into Zaire. [RG]
- Justice: ICTR in Arusha (high-level cases); Rwandan gacaca community courts (law 2001, closed 18 June 2012) tried 1,958,634 cases involving 1,003,227 people. [RG]
- **RPF killings (debate).** Robert Gersony's UNHCR team concluded the RPF had killed "between 25,000 and 45,000 persons" April–August 1994; his findings were suppressed by the UN and a summary leaked only in 2010. Alison Des Forges estimated ~30,000 killed by the RPF. Kagame told Stephen Kinzer killings were by "rogue soldiers". Kibeho camp massacre 1995: Australian UNAMIR soldiers estimated at least 4,000 killed; Rwandan government said 338. The Rwandan government denied the Gersony allegations; gacaca did not try RPF crimes. RG also notes the "double genocide" thesis — writer: present RPF killings as war crimes/reprisals documented by these sources, and note that "double genocide" framing is widely regarded as genocide denial (RG lists it under a see-also; the denial characterisation is NOT separately verified here). [RG]
- **Second Congo War ("Africa's World War"), 2 August 1998 – 18 July 2003:** began when Laurent-Désiré Kabila turned on his Rwandan and Ugandan backers; drew in nine African states and ~25 armed groups (Rwanda, Uganda, Burundi vs. Zimbabwe, Angola, Namibia, Chad and others). Deaths: IRC 5.4 million excess deaths August 1998–April 2007 (3.8 million to April 2004), mostly from disease and malnutrition; "deadliest conflict since World War II" (IRC 2008); at least 350,000 violent deaths by 2001. The Human Security Report Project (2010) argued the IRC used too low a baseline death rate and revised it to ~860,000 excess deaths. UN Panel of Experts (2001–02) accused Rwanda, Uganda and Zimbabwe of systematically exploiting diamonds, cobalt, coltan and gold. Violence continued in the Kivus and Ituri. [DRC2]

## 4. Chechnya
- First Chechen War 1994–96: Yeltsin's operation to "establish constitutional order"; Grozny captured March 1995; Dudayev killed April 1996; separatists retook Grozny August 1996 → Khasavyurt Accord, 1997 peace treaty. Russian military dead 5,500 official (independent 5,000–14,000); Chechen civilian deaths 30,000–100,000; more than 500,000 displaced. [CH1]
- Second Chechen War, August 1999–April 2009: incursion into Dagestan, then the September 1999 apartment bombings in Russian cities (over 300 dead; blamed on Chechens though no Chechen claimed them); siege of Grozny December 1999–February 2000; direct rule from May 2000. Total killed 50,000–80,000 (infobox; Amnesty: ~25,000 civilians killed + 5,000 missing). In 2003 the UN called Grozny "the most destroyed city on Earth". Moscow theatre siege 2002; Beslan school siege 2004, at least 334 dead. [CH2]

## 5. 9/11 and Afghanistan, 2001–2021
- 11 September 2001: 19 al-Qaeda hijackers (15 Saudi, 2 Emirati, 1 Egyptian, 1 Lebanese) seized four airliners; Twin Towers struck 8:46 and ~9:03 a.m., Pentagon 9:37, Flight 93 crashed near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, 10:03 after a passenger revolt. 2,977 victims (2,996 incl. hijackers), including 343 firefighters and 72 law-enforcement officers; citizens of more than 90 countries died; 9,000+ later deaths from related illness (infobox). Deadliest terrorist attack in history. NATO invoked Article 5 for the only time. [911]
- War in Afghanistan 2001–2021: US-led invasion (Operation Enduring Freedom) with the Northern Alliance toppled the Taliban's Islamic Emirate; the Taliban had refused to hand over bin Laden without evidence and offered handover to a neutral country, which the US dismissed. ISAF (UN-sanctioned); Taliban insurgency by 2003; foreign troops peaked ~140,000 under ISAF in 2011; bin Laden killed in Pakistan 2011; ISAF combat ended 2014; US–Taliban deal 2020; Taliban took Kabul 15 August 2021, Ghani fled; last US aircraft left 30 August 2021. Longest war in US history. [AFG]
- Afghanistan deaths: 176,000–212,000+ total, including 46,319 civilians (Costs of War); IPPNW/PSR estimate 106,000–170,000 civilians; 66,650 more in the related war in north-west Pakistan. UNAMA (counting from 2008) attributed most civilian casualties to anti-government elements (61–80% a year), but ~41% to pro-government forces in 2008 falling to ~18% in 2015. Afghan security forces: Ghani said 45,000+ killed 2014–Jan 2019. By the fall of Kabul 2.6 million Afghans remained refugees and 4 million internally displaced (after 5.7 million returns post-2001). IEDs became the Taliban's main weapon by 2009. [AFG]

## 6. Iraq, 2003–2011, and the counting of the dead
- Invasion began 20 March 2003 ("shock and awe"; US, UK, Australia, Poland). Rationale: claims that Iraq had WMD and supported al-Qaeda; the 9/11 Commission (2004) found no credible Saddam–al-Qaeda link and no WMD stockpiles were found. Saddam captured 2003, executed 2006. Sectarian civil war; 2007 "surge" (IRQ lead says "an additional 170,000 troops" — this is wrong as worded; ~170,000 was roughly the total US force level. Say "the 2007 troop surge" without a number). US withdrawal completed 2011; US re-engaged against ISIS from 2014. [IRQ]
- Coalition military dead 4,826 (4,508 US) (IRQ infobox). [IRQ]
- **Iraqi deaths (all disputed; attribute each):** Iraq Body Count (body count from media, NGO and official records): 186,901–210,296 violent civilian deaths, March 2003 onward (CIW, current table; IBC article "as of February 2019: 183,249–205,785"); IBC itself says even its highest totals "may be missing many civilian deaths". Iraq Family Health Survey: 151,000 violent deaths, March 2003–June 2006. *Lancet* 2004 (Burnham/Roberts et al.): ~98,000 excess deaths (95% CI 8,000–194,000) excluding the Fallujah outlier. *Lancet* 2006: 654,965 excess deaths (CI 392,979–942,636) to June 2006, 601,027 violent. *PLOS Medicine* 2013: ~461,000 deaths to June 2011, >60% violent. ORB 2007 poll: 1,033,000. AP: at least 110,600 violent deaths to April 2009. WikiLeaks Iraq War Logs (2004–09): 109,032 deaths incl. 66,081 civilians. IRQ lead summary: "150,000 to over a million deaths, including over 100,000 civilians". [CIW; LAN; IBC; IRQ]
- Criticism of the 2006 *Lancet* survey: AAPOR (3 February 2009) censured lead author Gilbert Burnham for refusing to disclose basic elements of the research. [LAN]
- Brown University's **Costs of War Project** (Watson Institute; founded 2010 by Catherine Lutz and Neta Crawford): post-9/11 wars in Iraq, Afghanistan, Pakistan, Syria, Yemen, Libya and Somalia directly killed 905,000–940,000 people and indirectly 3.6–3.8 million (total 4.5–4.7 million; indirect deaths derived from the Geneva Declaration ratio of four indirect deaths per direct death); 38 million displaced; US cost over $8 trillion. [COW]
- **Legality:** Kofi Annan, September 2004: "From our point of view and the UN Charter point of view, it [the war] was illegal." UK Attorney General Lord Goldsmith first advised the war would breach international law, then on 7 March 2003 shifted. Supporters cite UNSC Res. 678 and 1441 ("final opportunity"); France, Germany and Russia opposed. The UK Chilcot (Iraq Inquiry) report, 2016: the legal process was "unsatisfactory", the US and UK "undermined the authority of the United Nations", and the war was unnecessary as peaceful options were not exhausted. [LEG; IRQ]

## 7. The Islamic State: rise and fall
- Origins: al-Qaeda in Iraq → Mujahideen Shura Council (Jan 2006) → Islamic State of Iraq (15 Oct 2006) → ISIL (2013, after entering the Syrian war) → declared a worldwide caliphate in 2014. [IS]
- Fall of Mosul, 4–10 June 2014: ~3,000 attackers against an Iraqi army and police nominally 60,000 (hollowed by "ghost soldiers"); ~500,000 civilians fled. [FOM]
- At its peak (end 2015) the caliphate ruled about 12 million people, with a budget over $1 billion and 30,000+ fighters. [IS]
- Atrocities: genocides against Yazidis and Iraqi Turkmen; persecution of Christians, Shia and Mandaeans; filmed beheadings; destruction of cultural sites; attacks abroad including Paris (November 2015), Kerman, Iran (2024) and Crocus City Hall, Moscow (2024). [IS]
- **Yazidis** (Kurdish-speaking ethnoreligious group; call it the "74th firman"): from August 2014 (Sinjar), the UN reports ~5,000 Yazidis killed and ~10,800 women and girls trafficked; by 2015 over 71% of Yazidis displaced. US, UK and Australia airdrops to those trapped on Mount Sinjar; YPG and PKK opened an evacuation corridor; US airstrikes began. Recognised as genocide by the UN, Council of Europe, EU, US, Canada, Armenia, Iraq. [YAZ]
- Defeat: Battle of Mosul 16 October 2016 – victory declared 10 July 2017, "the toughest urban battle since World War II" (BOM); AP counted 9,606–11,000 Mosul residents killed; Amnesty attributed 5,805 civilian deaths (19 Feb–19 June 2017) to Iraqi/coalition strikes. Raqqa battle June–October 2017. Last territory lost at Baghuz, March 2019. Baghdadi's death announced by Trump 27 October 2019 (caption says 26 October — the raid was the night of 26–27 October; say "October 2019"). Affiliates retain territory in Africa (Sahel) as of 2025. [BOM; IS]

## 8. The Arab Spring and its wars
- Mohamed Bouazizi, a Tunisian fruit seller, set himself alight on 17 December 2010 after his wares were confiscated; he died 4 January 2011. Ben Ali fled to Saudi Arabia 14 January 2011; Mubarak resigned 11 February 2011 after 18 days; Gaddafi fell 2011; Saleh (Yemen) 2012. Slogan: *ash-shaʻb yurīd isqāṭ an-niẓām* ("the people want to bring down the regime"). Aftermath ("Arab Winter"): civil wars in Syria, Libya, Yemen; Egypt's Morsi elected then removed (2013; Rabaa massacre, August 2013). A "second Arab Spring" 2018–19 toppled Bashir (Sudan) and Bouteflika (Algeria). [AS]
- **Libya 2011:** protests in Benghazi from 15 February; UNSC Res. 1970 (26 Feb, ICC referral) and Res. 1973 (17 March: no-fly zone and "all necessary measures" to protect civilians); NATO bombing; Tripoli fell August; NTC recognised by UN 16 September; Gaddafi captured and killed at Sirte 20 October 2011. Russia and China, who abstained, said the operation went beyond its mandate into regime change. Deaths: NTC/AP estimate "at least 30,000" (September 2011); infobox component figures far lower — present as uncertain. Militias' refusal to disarm led to a second civil war. [LIB; R2P]
- **Syria:** protests from March 2011 met with lethal force; armed opposition (FSA); Iran (2013), Hezbollah and Russia (2015) intervened for Assad; Turkey, Qatar, the US backed rebels; US-led coalition bombed ISIS from 2014 and backed the Kurdish-led SDF; Turkey invaded the north from 2016. Aleppo retaken by the government in 2016. HTS offensive November 2024; Aleppo fell in three days; Assad fled to Moscow 8 December 2024 — "the war lasted almost 14 years"; Ahmed al-Sharaa (former HTS leader) named president January 2025; massacres of Alawites in March 2025 (SOHR: 1,614 civilians killed by pro-government militias, "actual numbers much higher"); Druze insurgency in Suwayda 2025. [SYR]
- Chemical weapons: Ghouta sarin attack, 21 August 2013, by Assad's forces; deaths estimated 281–1,729; UN inspectors found "clear and convincing evidence" of sarin; followed by an agreement to destroy Syria's arsenal that was not completed (later attacks Khan Shaykhun 2017, Douma 2018). [GHO]
- Syrian dead (attribute): UN (Sept 2021) documented at least 350,209 identified individuals killed March 2011–March 2021, "certainly an under-count"; OHCHR (28 June 2022) at least 306,887 civilians killed to March 2021 (~1.5% of pre-war population); UN envoy de Mistura (April 2016) "more than 400,000"; estimates ~656,493 total as of March 2025 (SOHR-based); worst year 2014 (~110,000). [SYR]
- Syrian refugees (attribute): pre-war population ~22 million; by 2016 >6 million internally displaced and ~5 million abroad; as of Dec 2022 6.7 million refugees and 13 million displaced overall; Turkey the largest host (3.6 million in 2019; almost 3 million at Assad's fall); almost one million asylum applications in Europe by August 2017; 6.2 million IDPs in 2022 (UNHCR). Alan Kurdi, a drowned Syrian child, became the crisis's symbol in September 2015. IOM (Dec 2025): over 782,075 Syrians returned from abroad since Assad's fall. [SRC; SYR]
- **Yemen:** Houthis took Sanaa September 2014; President Hadi fled; Saudi-led coalition intervened March 2015; widely seen as part of the Iran–Saudi proxy rivalry; the UAE backs the southern separatist STC; AQAP active. UN-brokered truce from 2 April 2022. UN: over 150,000 killed in fighting, plus estimates of more than 227,000 dead from famine and lack of health care; a UNDP projection (reported by Al Jazeera, 2021) of 377,000 deaths by end-2021 (state as a projection, ~60% indirect — the 60% split is from memory, NOT verified; omit). Cholera epidemic from 2017 (over 200,000 cases by 24 June 2017). [YEM]

## 9. Darfur and Sudan
- War in Darfur from February 2003: SLM and JEM rebels (largely from the non-Arab Fur, Zaghawa and Masalit) against the government, which responded with ethnic cleansing using the Janjaweed militia. Deaths: UN estimate 300,000; Sudanese government 10,000. Displaced: 2.85–3 million (UN). Colin Powell (2004) called it genocide; the UNSC referred Darfur to the ICC (US and China abstained); ICC warrants for Ahmed Haroun and Ali Kushayb (April 2007) and for President Omar al-Bashir (March 2009; genocide charges added later per DAR). [DAR]
- Sudanese civil war since 15 April 2023: SAF (al-Burhan) vs RSF (Hemedti, Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo; descended from the Janjaweed — that lineage is commonly stated but NOT checked here). As of 6 July 2026 over 14 million displaced, 4.5 million refugees — "one of the largest displacement crises in recent history"; deaths "highly uncertain, could be more than 150,000–400,000"; ~25 million facing severe food insecurity, famine confirmed in several regions. SAF retook Khartoum by March 2025. El Fasher, besieged from April 2024, fell to the RSF on 26 October 2025, followed by what some experts called a genocidal massacre: 60,000–70,000 deaths "by one estimate", satellite-based estimates as high as 150,000 — highly uncertain, flag clearly. Foreign involvement: UAE, Russia, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Egypt and others. [SUD]
- On 7 January 2025 US Secretary of State Blinken determined that the RSF and allied militias had committed genocide and sanctioned Hemedti. [WebSearch: https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/01/mil-250107-state02.htm ; https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7424781]

## 10. Russia's wars: Georgia, Crimea, Donbas, Ukraine
- **Georgia, August 2008:** after South Ossetian shelling of Georgian villages from 1 August, Georgia attacked Tskhinvali on the night of 7–8 August; Russia launched a full invasion on 8 August ("peace enforcement"); Sarkozy brokered a ceasefire 12 August; Russia recognised Abkhazia and South Ossetia 26 August. About 192,000 displaced (Georgian official: at least 230,000); Georgians ethnically cleansed from South Ossetia. Deaths (infobox, attributed): Georgia 180 security forces + 224 civilians (Georgian figures); Russia/separatists 163–170 military; South Ossetian civilians 162 (Russian figure) or 365 total (South Ossetian figure). First war combining cyber attacks with military action. EU (Tagliavini) report, September 2009: open hostilities began with "a large-scale Georgian military operation against the town of Tskhinvali" but "no way to assign overall responsibility for the conflict to one side alone"; Russia's response went beyond reasonable self-defence (that clause is NOT verified in today's text; omit or verify). [GEO]
- **Crimea, 2014:** after Yanukovych's ouster (22 February 2014; Euromaidan, almost 100 protesters killed by state forces), Russian special forces without insignia seized sites from 27 February (Putin later admitted they were Russian troops); a referendum held under occupation; Russia incorporated Crimea 18 March 2014 — first annexation of part of a European state by another since WWII. UNGA Res. 68/262 affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity: 100 for, 11 against, 58 abstentions. Russia suspended from the G8. [CRI; RUW]
- **Donbas, 2014–2022:** Russian-backed seizures from April 2014 (Girkin's unit took Sloviansk 12 April); DPR/LPR; covert Russian troops; MH17 shot down 17 July 2014 by DPR forces, 298 killed; Minsk (Sept 2014) and Minsk II (12 Feb 2015) agreements. UN: 14,200–14,400 killed 6 April 2014–31 December 2021 (≈6,500 separatist forces, 4,400 Ukrainian forces, 3,404 civilians); 1.6 million+ Ukrainians displaced (infobox). [DON; CRU]
- **Full-scale invasion, 24 February 2022:** Putin's "special military operation" to "demilitarise and denazify" Ukraine (claims RUW calls without evidence); fronts from Belarus, Crimea and the Donbas; retreat from around Kyiv by April 2022, Bucha massacre uncovered; Mariupol captured after siege; Ukrainian counteroffensives in Kharkiv and Kherson (autumn 2022); Russia "illegally annexed" four partly occupied provinces (Sept 2022); Ukraine's Kursk incursion August 2024, North Korean troops sent to help Russia. As of August 2026 Russia occupies roughly 20% of Ukraine. ICC arrest warrant for Putin 17 March 2023 (abduction/deportation of children). Ukraine accepted a US-backed 30-day ceasefire proposal in March 2025; Russia rejected calls for a ceasefire; November 2025 US 28-point peace plan (criticised as pro-Russian), later revised versions rejected by Russia; RUW: "Peace negotiations have stalled" (as of today's text). - **Ukraine casualties (attribute and date every figure; all provisional):** OHCHR verified 17,257 civilians killed and 53,693 injured in Ukraine 24 Feb 2022–31 Aug 2026, and believes the real number higher. ## 12. The changing character of war: insurgency, drones, targeted killing
- Targeted killing: "a form of assassination carried out by governments outside a judicial procedure or a battlefield"; defenders cite self-defence against terrorists and argue drones are "more humane and more accurate"; scholars are divided on effectiveness. US cases: bin Laden (2011), Ayman al-Zawahiri (2022), US citizen Anwar al-Awlaki and his teenage son (2011). [TK]
- BBC, citing the Bureau of Investigative Journalism: 2,243 US drone strikes in the first two years of Trump's presidency vs 1,878 in Obama's eight years (scope of countries not specified in TK — present with that caveat). A 2016 Obama order requiring annual reporting of civilian deaths outside combat zones was revoked in 2019. [TK]
- Pakistan: BIJ data (to ~2017): 429 CIA strikes, 2,514–4,023 killed, of whom 424–969 civilians and 172–207 children (secondary citation via Wikipedia snapshot; BIJ ranges; a Columbia Law clinic warned trackers may undercount civilians; US officials disputed civilian figures). [WebSearch: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_attacks_in_Pakistan ; https://hri.law.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/publications/COLUMBIACountingDronesFinal.pdf]
- Cheap drones spread: Azerbaijan used Turkish Bayraktar TB2 and Israeli Harop drones decisively against Armenia in the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war, which *The Economist* cited as indicating the future of warfare; in Ukraine, FPV drones dominate the front; Ukraine's Operation Spiderweb (1 June 2025) launched FPV drones from trucks smuggled into Russia against airbases including Belaya and Olenya. ## Debates
- **"New wars" (Kaldor).** Post-Cold War wars fought between shifting state and non-state networks, in the name of identity rather than ideology, controlling populations through fear, financed by predatory economies; related terms "wars among the people", "hybrid wars" (Frank Hoffman), Martin Shaw's "degenerate warfare"; supporters include Münkler and van Creveld. Critics: De Waal (these are wars in weakly governed states, not new wars); Duffield (network wars); Edward Newman (historical examples show much is not new, though Bosnia fits). [NEW]
- **Responsibility to protect.** ICISS report (December 2001, set up by Canada under Lloyd Axworthy in answer to Annan's question); unanimously endorsed at the 2005 World Summit (paras 138–139); three pillars; force only via the UNSC. Libya 2011 was the first Security Council-authorised intervention citing R2P; Russia and China said it became regime change, and later vetoed Syria resolutions. Rwanda and Srebrenica are its founding failures; Kosovo the "illegal but legitimate" precedent. [R2P; KOS; LIB]
- **Legality of the Iraq war.** Annan "illegal"; Goldsmith's shifting advice; Chilcot; supporters' reliance on Res. 678/1441; John Prescott (2016) came to agree with Annan. [LEG; IRQ]
- **Counting the dead.** Body counts (IBC, UNAMA, OHCHR — minimums) vs population surveys (*Lancet*, IFHS, PLOS) vs excess-mortality models (IRC Congo, Costs of War's 4:1 ratio). The Congo dispute (IRC 5.4 million vs Human Security Report ~860,000) and the *Lancet*/AAPOR controversy show how method drives results; all figures should be presented as ranges with sources. [CIW; LAN; DRC2; COW]
- **Drones and targeted killing.** Precision and force protection vs extrajudicial killing, civilian deaths undercounted, and secrecy (reporting order revoked 2019); now cheap drones in the hands of states and non-state actors alike (Karabakh, Ukraine). [TK; DW]
- **Has violence declined? (Pinker vs critics).** Pinker (2011): violence of all kinds has declined, "probably the most peaceful time in the history of the human species", owing to states, commerce, literacy, reason. Critics: Taleb and Cirillo (*Physica A*) find no decline in the lethality of war given fat-tailed data; Aaron Clauset (*Science Advances*, 2018) finds the "long peace" plausibly chance; Braumoeller (*Only the Dead*, 2019) finds no consistent trend except falling interstate uses of force after the Cold War, explained by international orders. Writer: the wars of this chapter (Congo, Syria, Ukraine, Sudan) are the test cases. [BA]
- **Rwanda: RPF crimes.** Gersony / Des Forges / Kibeho vs the Rwandan government's denials; the line between documenting RPF killings and "double genocide" denial. [RG]
- **Kosovo and Libya interventions** — humanitarian success or law-breaking precedents later cited by Russia (Crimea's "self-determination" claim). (The Russian citation of Kosovo is commonly reported but NOT verified here.) [KOS; CRI]
