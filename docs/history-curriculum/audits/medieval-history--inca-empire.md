**Kept with one confirming pass or low-risk:** "on a smaller scale than the Mexica" (sacrifice); no direct Inca–Aztec contact; Coricancha's gold sheets (A confirmed plates of gold; B confirmed sheets).

**Confirmed by both passes:** Tawantinsuyu and the four suyu; about ten million people; some 4,000 km from Ecuador to central Chile; no wheel, writing, or money for most purposes; the Chanka attack and Pachacuti's rebuilding of Cuzco; Topa Inca and Huayna Capac's conquests; tambos, stairways and grass bridges (Q'eswachaka, UNESCO 2013); khipus and khipukamayuq; the mit'a and reciprocity; qullqa storehouses; mitmaqkuna; cumbi cloth; Inti and the Sapa Inca; royal mummies and split inheritance (Conrad and Demarest 1984); huacas; capacocha and the Llullaillaco children (1999); Machu Picchu above the Urubamba, Bingham (1911) and the Yale returns (2010–2012); the deaths of Huayna Capac and his heir; the civil war of Huáscar and Atahualpa; Pizarro's 168 men; Cajamarca (16 November 1532); the ransom; Atahualpa's execution (July 1533); Manco's siege (1536–37) and Vilcabamba; Túpac Amaru (1572); and the reading list (D'Altroy 2014, MacQuarrie 2007, Hemming 1970, Urton 2017, Guaman Poma c. 1615).
