# Known issues to check in Phase 2

Leads found while researching new chapters that may affect articles already published. Each must be checked against the article during its Phase 2 review.

- **`renaissance-and-reformation--atlantic-slave-wars`** (Haitian Revolution section), from the French and Haitian Revolutions research (2026-11-11):
  - Bois Caïman is contested: there are no first-hand accounts, Boukman is first named in 1843, Hoffmann calls it a myth, and Geggus dates it 21 August. Check how it is presented.
  - "Napoleon's attempt to restore slavery" is acceptable, but if the text says he restored it in Martinique, note that the 1794 abolition was never applied there (the island was British-held).
  - Haiti as "first Black republic": it was formally an empire under Dessalines in 1804–1806. "First independent Black-led state" is safer.
  - Toussaint Louverture was free from about 1772–76, and himself owned land and enslaved people.

- **`renaissance-and-reformation--atlantic-slave-wars`**, from the Abolition and Emancipation research (2026-11-11):
  - If it mentions Harriet Tubman freeing "300", the sources give about 70 people on about 13 missions; the Combahee Ferry raid she guided freed more than 700.
  - If it quotes Sojourner Truth's "Ain't I a Woman?", that wording comes from Frances Gage's 1863 version, not the 1851 report.
  - Check the numbers for Demerara executions (27 or 33) and the Brazilians freed in 1888 (about 600,000 to 723,419).

## Other 20th- and 19th-century chapters
- The British Empire chapter calls Zimbabwe (1980) "the last British possession in Africa". Britain still administers the Chagos Islands (British Indian Ocean Territory); the 2025 treaty with Mauritius had not taken effect as of November 2026. Say "the last British colony on the African mainland". (Found by the Decolonization research, 2026-11-11.)
- The Second World War chapter says "over 60,000 leaders were arrested" in the Quit India movement; the Quit India Movement article gives over 100,000 arrests. Check and align. (Found by the Partition research, 2026-11-11.)
- The Latin America after Independence chapter says Menem pardoned "nearly 300" in 1989; the pardons came in 1989–90, with the junta leaders pardoned in December 1990. Say "in 1989–90" and re-check the figure. (Found by the Latin America in the Cold War research, 2026-11-11.)
- The 20th-century overview says the world was not as integrated as in 1914 again "until the 1980s"; the Deglobalization article says many rich countries regained pre-1914 trade ratios by the 1970s. Consider "the later twentieth century". (Found by the Globalization research, 2026-11-11.)
- The Interwar Period chapter's "world trade fell by more than half" (1929–32) is a fall in value; openness (trade/GDP) fell by about 30 per cent (Federico and Tena-Junguito). Make clear it is value. (Found by the Globalization research, 2026-11-11.)
- The 20th-century overview's "OPEC embargo … quadrupled oil prices": the 1973 embargo was imposed by OAPEC (the Arab producers); the quadrupling is right. Its "ending Bretton Woods" for 1971 is better as "effectively ended" (floating from 1973; Jamaica Accords 1976). Its claim that inequality "has trended [upward] since" is safer as "rose steeply and stayed high" (US top-1% shares roughly flat since 2007). (Found by the Neoliberal Age research, 2026-11-11.)
- The 20th-century overview's Rwanda figure "500,000–800,000 dead": the scholarly range is about 500,000–662,000 Tutsi, plus about 10,000 Twa and 10,000–30,000 Hutu; Rwanda's government says over 1 million. Consider "some 500,000–800,000 or more, mostly Tutsi". (Found by the Post-Cold War research, 2026-11-11.)
