# Known issues to check in Phase 2

Leads found while researching new chapters that may affect articles already published. Each must be checked against the article during its Phase 2 review.

- **`renaissance-and-reformation--atlantic-slave-wars`** (Haitian Revolution section), from the French and Haitian Revolutions research (2026-11-11):
  - Bois Caïman is contested: there are no first-hand accounts, Boukman is first named in 1843, Hoffmann calls it a myth, and Geggus dates it 21 August. Check how it is presented.
  - "Napoleon's attempt to restore slavery" is acceptable, but if the text says he restored it in Martinique, note that the 1794 abolition was never applied there (the island was British-held).
  - Haiti as "first Black republic": it was formally an empire under Dessalines in 1804–1806. "First independent Black-led state" is safer.
  - Toussaint Louverture was free from about 1772–76, and himself owned land and enslaved people.

- **`renaissance-and-reformation--atlantic-slave-wars`**, from the Abolition and Emancipation research (2026-11-11):
  - If it mentions Harriet Tubman freeing "300", the sources give about 70 people on about 13 missions; the Combahee Ferry raid she guided freed more than 700.
  - If it quotes Sojourner Truth's "Ain't I a Woman?", that wording comes from Frances Gage's 1863 version, not the 1851 report.
  - Check the numbers for Demerara executions (27 or 33) and the Brazilians freed in 1888 (about 600,000 to 723,419).
