# Philosophy: known issues in published chapters

Found after publication; fix in a later batch.

### Socrates and the Sophists — open
- "Plato … about twenty-five at the trial": follows Nails's birth date (424/3). SEP Plato (Kraut, rev. 2026) gives 429?; IEP 428/7; Diogenes Laertius says 28. Say "in his mid-to-late twenties". Source: research/ancient--plato.md.

### History of Philosophy overview (pre-plan article, not yet audited) — open
- "first complete philosophical system" (Plato) and the Parmenides' "most devastating criticisms of the Forms ever produced": present as debated (SEP Plato: exploratory, incompletely systematic; Meinwald). Source: research/ancient--plato.md.
- Al-Fārābī "harmonized Plato's political philosophy with Islam": the harmonisation treatise's authorship is questioned (Rashed 2009); full Arabic Republic doubtful. Source: research/ancient--plato.md.
- Parmenides "set the task for every successor": one reading among several (Palmer). Source: research/ancient--presocratics.md.
- Confucius 551–479 BCE: mark as traditional dates; "earlier histories routinely began philosophy in Egypt, Persia, or India" — Persia unsourced, "routinely" overstated. Source: research/what-is-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Aristotle's syllogistic "unrivaled until the nineteenth century": Stoic propositional logic (Chrysippus) was a real rival in antiquity; say "dominant from late antiquity to the nineteenth century". Source: research/ancient--aristotle.md.
- Overview: Upanishads "c. 800–300 BCE" and ātman = brahman as their "central" identification. IEP (Black) gives c. 700–300 BCE; Olivelle: earliest 7th–6th c. BCE ± a century; the identification is one teaching among several. Source: research/ancient--upanishads.md.
- Overview: Hadot's "way of life" thesis "fits this era exactly" — present as contested (Cooper 2012); "the walled Garden" (Epicurus) unsupported. Source: research/ancient--hellenistic-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Confucianism "synthesized with Buddhist metaphysics as Neo-Confucianism" — SEP presents Neo-Confucianism as an anti-Buddhist revival that reinterpreted the classics with Buddhist-influenced concepts. Source: research/ancient--confucianism.md.
- Overview: the Buddha's "there is no enduring self" with craving as the cause of suffering stated flatly — present the debate over what non-self denies and IEP's point that ignorance is the deeper root. Source: research/ancient--early-buddhism.md.
- Overview: Mohism as "history's first consequentialism" — matches Fraser, but name the rival divine-command reading; the Zhuangzi fish debate is from an Outer Chapter (ch. 17), possibly not by Zhuangzi. Source: research/ancient--hundred-schools.md.
- Overview: Neoplatonism's "emanation" — gloss as procession and return (SEP neoplatonism calls "emanation" misleading); don't call the school closed in 529 Plato's Academy. Source: research/medieval--neoplatonism.md.
- Overview: Nāgārjuna's tetralemmas "read like proto-deconstruction" — an interpretation; present as such. Source: research/medieval--mahayana-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Maimonides "pioneering negative theology" — overstated: SEP credits Philo as founder of negative theology in monotheism, and Saadia already used a via negativa; say "the most systematic medieval exponent". Source: research/medieval--jewish-philosophy.md.

### The Upanishads and the Beginnings of Indian Philosophy — open
- "Rāmānuja (c. 1000)" fits only the traditional dating (1017–1137; Carman 1077–1157); "Madhva (c. 1200)" fits only the minority dating (1238–1317 more usual; 1199–1278). Use "eleventh–twelfth century" and "thirteenth century". Source: research/medieval--vedanta.md.
- Overview: al-Ghazālī's arguments "occasionalist" and "strikingly anticipate Hume" — present the occasionalism debate (Marmura/Frank/Griffel) and the differences from Hume. Source: research/medieval--ghazali-and-averroes.md.
