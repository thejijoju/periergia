### History of Philosophy overview (pre-plan article, not yet audited) — fixed 19 Nov 2026
- "first complete philosophical system" (Plato) and the Parmenides' "most devastating criticisms of the Forms ever produced": present as debated (SEP Plato: exploratory, incompletely systematic; Meinwald). Source: research/ancient--plato.md.
- Al-Fārābī "harmonized Plato's political philosophy with Islam": the harmonisation treatise's authorship is questioned (Rashed 2009); full Arabic Republic doubtful. Source: research/ancient--plato.md.
- Parmenides "set the task for every successor": one reading among several (Palmer). Source: research/ancient--presocratics.md.
- Confucius 551–479 BCE: mark as traditional dates; "earlier histories routinely began philosophy in Egypt, Persia, or India" — Persia unsourced, "routinely" overstated. Source: research/what-is-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Aristotle's syllogistic "unrivaled until the nineteenth century": Stoic propositional logic (Chrysippus) was a real rival in antiquity; say "dominant from late antiquity to the nineteenth century". Source: research/ancient--aristotle.md.
- Overview: Upanishads "c. 800–300 BCE" and ātman = brahman as their "central" identification. IEP (Black) gives c. 700–300 BCE; Olivelle: earliest 7th–6th c. BCE ± a century; the identification is one teaching among several. Source: research/ancient--upanishads.md.
- Overview: Hadot's "way of life" thesis "fits this era exactly" — present as contested (Cooper 2012); "the walled Garden" (Epicurus) unsupported. Source: research/ancient--hellenistic-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Confucianism "synthesized with Buddhist metaphysics as Neo-Confucianism" — SEP presents Neo-Confucianism as an anti-Buddhist revival that reinterpreted the classics with Buddhist-influenced concepts. Source: research/ancient--confucianism.md.
- Overview: the Buddha's "there is no enduring self" with craving as the cause of suffering stated flatly — present the debate over what non-self denies and IEP's point that ignorance is the deeper root. Source: research/ancient--early-buddhism.md.
- Overview: Mohism as "history's first consequentialism" — matches Fraser, but name the rival divine-command reading; the Zhuangzi fish debate is from an Outer Chapter (ch. 17), possibly not by Zhuangzi. Source: research/ancient--hundred-schools.md.
- Overview: Neoplatonism's "emanation" — gloss as procession and return (SEP neoplatonism calls "emanation" misleading); don't call the school closed in 529 Plato's Academy. Source: research/medieval--neoplatonism.md.
- Overview: Nāgārjuna's tetralemmas "read like proto-deconstruction" — an interpretation; present as such. Source: research/medieval--mahayana-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Maimonides "pioneering negative theology" — overstated: SEP credits Philo as founder of negative theology in monotheism, and Saadia already used a via negativa; say "the most systematic medieval exponent". Source: research/medieval--jewish-philosophy.md.
- Overview: "Averroes's Aristotle ignited the thirteenth century" — oversimplified: Latin Averroes arrived c. 1228–30, and Aristotle also came directly from Greek (James of Venice, later Moerbeke). Source: research/medieval--early-scholasticism.md.
- Overview: Ockham's razor as "do not multiply entities beyond necessity" — that wording is a later slogan (popularly credited to John Punch, 1639); Ockham's attested lines are "Numquam ponenda est pluralitas sine necessitate" etc. Also Scotus is placed in the fourteenth century; he died 1308 (mostly 13th c.). Source: research/medieval--scotus-and-ockham.md.
- Overview: Descartes' interaction "unexplained at the pineal gland ever since" — leans on the caricature; the archived SEP pineal-gland entry doubts Descartes was a straightforward interactionist, and Elisabeth's objection and his 'union' reply should be named. Source: research/early-modern--descartes.md.
- Overview: scepticism "weaponized by Montaigne" — frame as Popkin's (contested) sceptical-crisis thesis. Source: research/early-modern--renaissance-philosophy.md.
- Overview: Spinoza's mind and body as "two aspects" — say "two attributes" (Ethics II P7). Source: research/early-modern--spinoza.md.
- Overview: Leibniz's "possible-worlds machinery … tools logic still uses" — SEP: he did not explicitly formulate possible-worlds semantics, though his account translates into it; soften. Source: research/early-modern--leibniz.md.
- Overview: Wollstonecraft as "the first systematic philosophical feminism" — Astell (and Gournay, 1622) wrote earlier; soften. Source: research/early-modern--enlightenment-and-rousseau.md.
- Overview: old metaphysics of God, soul and world "collapses into antinomies" — only the world yields antinomies (soul → paralogisms; God → failed proofs); and "Copernican revolution" in quotation marks is not Kant's phrase. Source: research/early-modern--kant.md.
- Overview: Kierkegaard's "leap of faith" (not his phrase; McKinnon) and "truth as subjectivity" (the pseudonym Climacus's), and Schopenhauer "importing … Buddhist thought" (he denied Buddhist influence before 1818; the Indian-influence question is debated) — qualify. Source: research/nineteenth--schopenhauer-and-kierkegaard.md.
- Overview: the harm principle as "the reference point of every free-speech debate" (Mill never names a 'harm principle'; SEP says ch. II partly qualifies it), and *The Subjection of Women* credited to Mill alone (Harriet Taylor Mill's role is debated); Bentham not mentioned. Source: research/nineteenth--utilitarianism.md.
- Says the later Wittgenstein "repudiated his earlier self" and that "meaning is use" without qualification; sources treat the break as a live debate and PI §43 limits use to "a large class of cases". Source: docs/philosophy-curriculum/research/twentieth--wittgenstein.md.
- Says the verification principle "failed its own test" (self-refutation is disputed; the decisive failures were technical: Church 1949, Hempel), that Quine "dissolved" the analytic–synthetic distinction (not accepted by current scholarship; Quine later allowed a limited analyticity), and implies holism replaced foundationalism (Neurath was already anti-foundationalist). Source: docs/philosophy-curriculum/research/twentieth--logical-positivism.md.
- Calls Foucault's *History of Madness* a "genealogy"; SEP: genealogy begins with *Discipline and Punish*, the earlier books are "archaeology". Source: docs/philosophy-curriculum/research/twentieth--structuralism-and-after.md.
- Fix: full audit, see audits/history--overview.md
