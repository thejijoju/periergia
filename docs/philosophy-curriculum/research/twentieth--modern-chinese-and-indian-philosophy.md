## B. China: the New Culture and May Fourth movements
- The May Fourth demonstrations (Beijing, 4 May 1919) protested the Versailles decision to transfer Germany's Shandong rights to Japan; students demonstrated and burned the houses of officials involved. [source: IEP-MCP §4b; WP-May_Fourth_Movement]
- The demonstrations marked a turning point in the broader anti-traditional **New Culture Movement (1915–1921)**; Chen Duxiu's own WP article gives "c. 1915–1922". [source: WP-May_Fourth_Movement; WP-Chen_Duxiu]
- Chen Duxiu (9 Oct 1879 – 27 May 1942) founded *Youth Magazine* (*Qingnian zazhi*) in Shanghai in September 1915, later retitled *New Youth* (*Xin Qingnian*, French subtitle *La Jeunesse*); he became Dean of Arts and Letters at Peking University. Hu Shi, Li Dazhao, Lu Xun, Qian Xuantong and others joined the editorial board in January 1918. [source: WP-Chen_Duxiu; WP-New_Culture_Movement]
- Chen called for "Mr. Confucius" to be replaced by "Mr. Science" (*Sài xiānsheng*) and "Mr. Democracy" (*Dé xiānsheng*). IEP-MCP writes "Mr. Science and Ms. Democracy" — a slip; use "Mr." for both. [source: WP-New_Culture_Movement; WP-May_Fourth_Movement; IEP-MCP §4b]
- Chen held Confucianism incompatible with science and democracy; after an anarchist phase he embraced Marxism and co-founded the Chinese Communist Party in 1921. [source: IEP-MCP §4d; WP-Chen_Duxiu]
- Hu Shi (Hu Shih, 17 Dec 1891 – 24 Feb 1962): studied with Dewey at Columbia; translated "pragmatism" as *shiyan zhuyi* ("experimental-ism"); argued in *New Youth* (January 1917, "A Preliminary Discussion of Literature Reform") for vernacular literature; published the first volume of *An Outline History of Chinese Philosophy* in 1919, never finished; maxim "make hypotheses boldly, but verify them carefully". Feng Youlan criticised the *Outline* for judging the schools from a pragmatist standpoint. [source: WP-Hu_Shih; IEP-MCP §4c; IEP-FENG §3]
- "Problems and isms" (summer 1919): Hu urged "More study of problems, less talk of isms"; Li Dazhao answered that problems could not be solved without a "fundamental solution". (The published *Modern China* chapter translates it "more study of questions, less study of isms"; both are translations.) [source: WP-Li_Dazhao (citing Meisner 1967)]
- Lu Xun (pen name of Zhou Shuren; 25 Sep 1881 – 19 Oct 1936): "Diary of a Madman" (1918, *New Youth*), the first story in modern vernacular Chinese; read Nietzsche in Tokyo from 1906. Already in the published *Nietzsche* and *Modern China* chapters (iron-house image); a sentence only. [source: WP-Lu_Xun]
- He-Yin Zhen (c. 1884 – c. 1920), anarchist and feminist, published in Tokyo in *Tianyi bao* ("Natural Justice", 1907–08); her 1907 essay "On the Question of Women's Liberation" argued that society could not be free without the liberation of women, and that liberation had to be achieved by women, not granted by men; she used *nannü* ("men and women") for a socially constructed hierarchy. A genuine woman thinker of the period. [source: WP-He-Yin_Zhen (citing Liu, Karl and Ko 2013; Zarrow 1988)]

## C. The 1923 "Science and Metaphysics" (*kexue yu xuanxue*) debate — ARGUMENT
- **Occasion:** in February 1923 (14 February per a WS source) Zhang Junmai (Carsun Chang, 18 Jan 1887 – 23 Feb 1969), a disciple of Liang Qichao who had studied with Rudolf Eucken at Jena (from January 1921) and been influenced by Bergson, lectured at the Qinghua (Tsinghua) School, Beijing, on "outlooks on life" (*rensheng guan*). [source: IEP-ZJM §§1–2; IEP-MCP §4e; WS-4]
- **Zhang's argument, step by step** (IEP-ZJM §2; IEP-MCP §4e):
  1. Science studies its objects by fixed method and seeks general laws; it is objective, analytic and causal.
  2. An "outlook on life" (defined, after Eucken, as "the observations, holds, hopes, and demands that I have toward the persons and the objects external to myself") is subjective, intuitive, synthetic, grounded in free will and unique to each person; each person "ought to develop his own outlook on life according to what his heart-mind (xin) tells him".
  3. The human mind is "in perpetual transformation" (after Bergson) and cannot be divided into fixed states; so science cannot predict the future of humanity (which is why he rejected Marxism).
  4. Therefore the problems of life cannot be solved by science; they belong to philosophy and moral cultivation, where Song–Ming Neo-Confucianism has much to offer ("Neo-Song Learning", *xin songxue*).
- **Objection (Ding Wenjiang, 20 Mar 1887 – 5 Jan 1936, geologist):** his reply, "Metaphysics and Science" (*Xuanxue yu kexue*; the WP article renders the title "Mythology and Science"), in *Nuli zhoubao* (*Endeavour Weekly*), April 1923 (one WS source says June). He accused Zhang of mixing Bergson's intuitionism with Wang Yangming's and of "recalling the specter of metaphysics in a positivist age"; science is "all-sufficient" in subject matter and method; it excludes subjective prejudice, whereas metaphysics posits a supersensible world "constructed from empty words". [source: IEP-MCP §4e; WP-Ding_Wenjiang; WS-4]
- **Reply (Zhang):** there is knowledge outside science (philosophy, religion) that science's criteria cannot verify; science is limited in scope and method. [source: IEP-MCP §4e]
- **Arbitration (Liang Qichao, "The View of Life and Science"):** most "problems of life" can be helped by science, and Zhang overstated intuition; but beauty, love, religious experience and moral sentiment can never be handled by scientific method. [source: IEP-MCP §4e]
- **Sides:** with Zhang: Liang Qichao, Liang Shuming, Zhang Dongsun; with Ding: Hu Shi, Chen Duxiu, Wu Zhihui and others. The debate lasted more than a year; the papers were collected at the end of 1923 as *Kexue yu renshengguan* (*Science and the Philosophy of Life*), with a preface by Hu Shi. IEP-MCP judges that Ding's "scientific" side prevailed. [source: IEP-MCP §4e; WS-4 (collection, Hu preface); a Chen Duxiu preface is NOT verified]
- **Significance:** IEP-ZJM: the debate "made a strong philosophical impression on an entire generation" and marks Zhang as a forerunner of New Confucianism; Hu Shi held that the difference between humans and animals is quantitative, Zhang that it is qualitative. [source: IEP-ZJM intro, §2]

## D. Liang Shuming and Xiong Shili
- Liang Shuming (18 Oct 1893 – 23 Jun 1988), recruited in 1917 by Cai Yuanpei to teach at Peking University; *Eastern and Western Cultures and Their Philosophies* (*Dongxi wenhua jiqi zhexue*), from his lectures, released 1921 (SEP-MC and WP; IEP-MCP says 1922). Influences: Bergson and Yogācāra. [source: SEP-MC §1; WP-Liang_Shuming; IEP-MCP §5c]
- Liang's thesis: three cultural orientations of will — the West outward-striving (conquering nature), China harmonising/moderate, India turning backward (renouncing desire); China reached the second stage "precociously" without passing through the first. IEP-MCP (translator unnamed) quotes: "it is necessary to reject Indian culture as useless, to modify Western culture with true happiness in view, and to reassert the value of Chinese culture", and "The world culture will eventually be the renovated Chinese culture." [source: IEP-MCP §5c; WP-Liang_Shuming]
- Liang led rural reconstruction in Zouping County, Shandong (1931–37); helped found the China Democratic League; criticised Marxist class analysis of rural China; in 1974 refused to join the "Criticize Lin, Criticize Confucius" campaign. Guy Alitto called him "the last Confucian". [source: WP-Liang_Shuming]
- Xiong Shili (1885 – 23 May 1968): poor rural Hubei family; revolutionary in the 1911 period; studied Yogācāra at the China Institute for Inner Learning (Ouyang Jingwu) from 1920; taught at Peking University from 1922; *New Treatise on the Uniqueness of Consciousness* (*Xin weishi lun*) published 1932 in classical Chinese (SEP-MC; "sometimes considered the first work of modern Confucianism proper"), rewritten in vernacular 1944 (WP; IEP-MCP gives only 1944). Remained in China after 1949; attacked in the Cultural Revolution; died in Shanghai. [source: SEP-MC §1; WP-Xiong_Shili; IEP-MCP §5f]
- Xiong's central thesis: original reality (*ti*, substance) and the phenomenal world (*yong*, function) are one, not two realms, against Yogācāra's separation; reality is perpetual transformation of "closing" (matter) and "opening" (mind). Criticised Buddhists (Dharmapāla) for treating evil as innate. [source: WP-Xiong_Shili; IEP-MCP §5f; SEP-MC §4.1]

## E. Feng Youlan (1895–1990)
- Born 4 Dec 1895, Tanghe County, Henan; died 26 Nov 1990, Beijing. Studied philosophy at Peking University 1915–18; Columbia from 1919 with Dewey; PhD 1923, thesis *A Comparative Study of Life Ideals*; later taught at Tsinghua (chair of philosophy 1934–38 and 1946–49 per WP; IEP says chair and dean from 1927). [source: IEP-FENG §1; WP-Feng_Youlan]
- *History of Chinese Philosophy* (*Zhongguo zhexue shi*): SEP-MC dates it "1931 and 1934" (two volumes); WP and IEP-FENG give 1934 for the complete work. English translation by Derk Bodde; IEP-FENG lists Princeton UP 1952–53 (IEP-MCP says the first volume appeared in English in 1937). It was the first major history to treat Chinese thought under the common category *zhexue* ("philosophy"); criticised for favouring Confucianism over Buddhism and Daoism. [source: SEP-MC §1; IEP-FENG §3; IEP-MCP §5d; WP-Feng_Youlan]
- *A Short History of Chinese Philosophy* (1948), from lectures at the University of Pennsylvania, ed. Derk Bodde. [source: IEP-FENG §3 and refs]
- **New Rational Philosophy** (*Xin lixue*, 1939), first of the "Six Books of Zhengyuan" (1939–46/47): a realist reconstruction of Cheng–Zhu Neo-Confucianism using *li* (principle), *qi* (material force), the substance of Dao (*daoti*) and the Great Whole (*daquan*); the True Realm of *li* is ontologically, not temporally, "first". [source: IEP-FENG §2b; IEP-MCP §5d; WP-Feng_Youlan]
- Visit to the Soviet Union en route to a Prague conference: 1934 (IEP-FENG) or 1935 (WP); briefly detained by the Nationalists afterwards. [source: IEP-FENG §1; WP-Feng_Youlan]
- **Maoist era:** he told Mao he was "unwilling to be a remnant of a bygone age in a time of greatness" and reinterpreted his work through Marxism; published "A Self-criticism of my New Philosophy of Principles" in *People's Daily*, 11 October 1950 (WS-6, from a Cambridge *Modern Asian Studies* bibliography); argued that Confucian *ren* could still be valuable if taken "abstractly"; denounced in the Cultural Revolution; then, 1973–76, assisted Jiang Qing's campaign against Confucius, which cost him his reputation and briefly his freedom after 1976. Began a Marxist *New History of Chinese Philosophy* in the 1980s, unfinished at his death. [source: IEP-FENG §1; WS-6]
- Wing-tsit Chan: "At a time when Chinese intellectuals saw little value in the Chinese tradition, Professor Feng upheld it." [source: IEP-FENG intro]

## F. New Confucianism and the 1958 Manifesto
- Terms: "modern", "contemporary" and "New Confucianism" translate *dangdai xin ruxue* / *xiandai xin rujia*; distinct from "Neo-Confucianism" (Song–Ming). Modern Confucianism is often called the "third major era" of Confucian thought (Mou; Tu Weiming). [source: SEP-MC intro]
- The core group: Xiong Shili and his "followers" Mou Zongsan, Tang Junyi and Xu Fuguan. SEP-MC warns that "disciples of Xiong" is "a little misleading": Mou called Xiong his teacher but criticised him; Tang said he did not understand Xiong well and once named Fang Dongmei as his teacher; Xu met Xiong only during WWII as an army officer. (WP's claim that all four Manifesto authors, including Zhang Junmai, were Xiong's students is wrong for Zhang.) [source: SEP-MC §1; WP-A_Manifesto…]
- The Manifesto: "A Manifesto for a Re-appraisal of Sinology and Reconstruction of Chinese Culture" (Chinese title *Wei Zhongguo wenhua jinggao shijie renshi xuanyan*, also translated "Declaration on Behalf of Chinese Culture Respectfully Announced to the People of the World"), published 1958 in the journals *Democratic Critique* (*Minzhu pinglun*) and *National Renaissance*; signed by Zhang Junmai, Tang Junyi, Mou Zongsan and Xu Fuguan; drafted by Tang with comments from the others. The phrase "New Confucian Manifesto" never occurs in it. English text in de Bary and Lufrano, *Sources of Chinese Tradition* vol. 2 (2000), pp. 550–555. [source: WP-A_Manifesto…; WP-New_Confucianism; SEP-MC §1]
- Content: Western sinology misread China (missionaries, antiquarian sinologists, present-minded political historians); understanding requires "sympathy and reverence"; the core is the doctrine of mind-nature (*xinxing*), following Lu Xiangshan and Wang Yangming rather than Zhu Xi; China must learn science and democracy from the West, and the West must learn from China, including "a more all-encompassing wisdom". [source: WP-A_Manifesto…; WP-New_Confucianism]
- The term "New Confucianism" was first used in 1963 (two articles in the Hong Kong journal *Rensheng*) and came into common use in the late 1970s. [source: WP-New_Confucianism]
- Tang Junyi (17 Jan 1909 – 2 Feb 1978): left for Hong Kong in 1949; a founder of New Asia College (with Qian Mu, Mou, Xu, per WP); works include *Cultural Consciousness and Moral Reason* (1958), *Origins of Chinese Philosophy* (1966–73), *Life Existence and the Horizons of Mind* (1977). "Culture is the manifestation or creation of human spiritual activity." Democracy grounded in equality of persons and the universal moral heart-mind; even the desire for power tacitly recognises others' wills. [source: SEP-MC §§1, 2.2, 5.2; WP-New_Confucianism]
- Xu Fuguan (born 1903 per SEP-MC and WP text; WP infobox 31 Jan 1904; died 1 Apr 1982): Nationalist army officer, rose to general and aide to Chiang Kai-shek; retired 1946; taught at Tunghai University, Taiwan; forced out 1969 for criticising the government; *A History of Chinese Theories of Human Nature* (1963), *The Spirit of Chinese Art* (1966). For Xu, democracy fully realises the Confucian ideal of "rule by virtue" by removing reliance on the ruler's personal virtue. [source: SEP-MC §§1, 5.3; WP-Hsu_Fu-kuan]
- Tu Weiming (b. 6 Feb 1940, Kunming): studied at Tunghai under Mou and Xu (WP adds Tang); Harvard–Yenching professor; "third epoch" of Confucianism, answering Joseph Levenson's claim that Confucianism had faded with the society that nourished it; "anthropocosmic" unity; *Centrality and Commonality* (1989); criticised by other Chinese Confucians for using Western religious terms. Now at Peking University. [source: IEP-TU; WP-Tu_Weiming]
- After 1978: Confucianism revived on the mainland; new editions of the overseas New Confucians were published (usually excising their criticisms of the Communist Party); "Mainland New Confucians" led by Jiang Qing advocate a "Political Confucianism" and a Confucian state, against Mou's line; Confucian meritocracy (Bai, Bell, Jiang) is a later anti-democratic alternative. [source: SEP-MC §§5, 6; WP-New_Confucianism]

## G. Mou Zongsan (1909–1995)
- Born 12 June 1909, Qixia, Shandong; died 12 April 1995 in Taiwan (SEP-MC). Peking University: WP says college prep 1927, undergraduate 1929; SEP-MC says he moved to Beijing and enrolled in 1927; IEP-MOU says he enrolled in the philosophy department in 1929. Met Xiong Shili there; graduated 1933. To Taiwan 1949; Hong Kong 1960 (invited by Tang Junyi); retired from the Chinese University of Hong Kong 1974. [source: WP-Mou_Tsung-san; SEP-MC §1; IEP-MOU §1]
- Translated all three of Kant's *Critiques* into Chinese (from English, per WP). Major works: *Substance of Mind and Substance of Human Nature* (*Xinti yu xingti*, 1968–69, 3 vols.), *Intellectual Intuition and Chinese Philosophy* (1971), *Phenomenon and Thing-in-Itself* (1975), *On the Highest Good* (*Yuanshan lun*, 1985). Collected Works: 33 volumes. SEP-MC: these works "made him widely acclaimed as the greatest Chinese philosopher of the twentieth century." [source: SEP-MC §1; WP-Mou_Tsung-san; IEP-MOU intro]
- "Three lineages" (*san xi*): beside Zhu Xi's school of principle and the Lu–Wang school of mind, a third line (Hu Hong, Liu Zongzhou) which Mou judged the true orthodoxy; Zhu Xi a well-meaning "usurper" whose heavenly principle was too transcendent; Confucian message as "immanent transcendence" (*neizai chaoyue*). Note for consistency with the *Beyond Europe* chapter: Mou ranked Lu–Wang above Zhu, but put Hu–Liu highest. [source: IEP-MOU §2c; SEP-MC §4]
- **Intellectual intuition** (*zhi de zhijue*): Kant reserved it to God; Mou held this "Kant's greatest mistake" and that Confucians, Buddhists and Daoists all affirm that humans have it; the Confucian form (*ren*, *liangzhi* "innate moral knowing") is moral and creative. Example: Mencius's child at the well — the sudden alarm is an occurrence of intellectual intuition. [source: IEP-MOU §3a; SEP-MC §3.3]
- **Mou's argument for autonomy (SEP-MC's reconstruction):** (1) Autonomy is necessary for morality to be possible. (2) Morality is a reality — we feel it: moral responses are "a definite presence which cannot be denied". (3) Therefore autonomy is a reality. This reverses Kant, for whom freedom is a postulate that cannot be known. **Objections (SEP-MC):** the intuitive method may mistake culturally learned responses for a priori ones (Tang: one should be filial "even if my parents do not love me"); Mou does not say how to tell true intellectual intuition from what merely feels like it (Fung 2021); and the argument persuades only those who accept the Kantian premise that morality requires autonomy. [source: SEP-MC §3.3]
- **Two-level ontology** (*liangceng cunyoulun*), modelled on the *Awakening of Faith*'s "one mind, two gates": "ontology without grasping" (mind knowing things-in-themselves) and "ontology with grasping" (the cognitive mind, *renzhi xin*, which apprehends objects in space, time and causation). The lower level arises through mind's "self-negation" (*ziwo kanxian*). [source: IEP-MOU §3b]
- **Perfect teaching** (*yuanjiao*), borrowed from Tiantai Buddhist doctrinal classification (*panjiao*); answers the problem of the highest good (coincidence of virtue and happiness) without God or an afterlife. Critics say Mou collapses happiness into virtue. [source: IEP-MOU §3c]

### ARGUMENT: "self-negation" (kanxian) of moral knowing as the ground of democracy and science
- Set out from SEP-MC §5.1 and IEP-MOU §§2a, 3b:
  1. Confucianism excels at "inner sagehood" (*neisheng*, moral cultivation) but was historically weak in "outer kingship" (*waiwang*: government, economy, science). [IEP-MOU §2a]
  2. Traditional Confucian politics assumed a *direct* connection (*zhitong*) between morality and politics: a morally good ruler is necessary and sufficient for good government. But it had no system for reliably passing power to sages; the system was "inherently unstable". [SEP-MC §5.1]
  3. Democracy is not a moral system: elections are not limited to the virtuous; its virtue is embodied in objective, value-neutral structures, which only theoretical ("constructive") reason, not moral reason, can produce. [SEP-MC §5.1; on the "constructive presentation of reason", SEP-MC §2.1]
  4. So moral reason must *restrict* or *negate itself* (*ziwo kanxian*) to let theoretical reason and partly autonomous politics develop: an *indirect* (*qutong*) connection. The same self-negation of moral knowing produces the cognitive mind that does science. [SEP-MC §5.1; IEP-MOU §3b]
  5. The foundation is Mencius's doctrine that human nature is good: democracy is necessary for realising the Confucian goal that each person realise their good nature. [SEP-MC §5.1]
  6. Conclusion: democracy and science are not alien to Confucianism but required by it — though the necessity is "dialectical or practical", not logical: there is no contradiction in an autocrat letting people realise their nature; "it simply does not often work out". [SEP-MC §5.1]
- **Objections:** (a) Traditional Confucians reject Mou's "wholesale acceptance of Western liberty and democracy" (WP-Mou_Tsung-san); mainland Confucians (Jiang Qing) and meritocrats (Bell, Bai) reject the democratic conclusion (SEP-MC §5). (b) Because the necessity is only practical, the argument shows at most that democracy is the likeliest means — the Confucian could choose a better meritocracy if one existed (SEP-MC frames this as the meritocrats' alternative; inference flagged as ours). (c) IEP-MOU's general criticisms: irrationalism (privileged intuition propounded rather than argued), essentialism about "Chinese culture", and "academicization" — a Confucianism confined to philosophy departments, a "lost soul". (d) Scholars agree Mou altered Kant's terms significantly; how consciously is disputed (IEP-MOU §2a). WS-5 found that Chinese scholarship (e.g. Yang Zebo 2023) debates *kanxian* extensively, but no English objection could be verified beyond these.
- **Replies:** Mou's point is that self-restriction is itself a moral demand: morality realises its own goals better through objective institutions than through hoping for sage-rulers (SEP-MC §5.1); Tang and Xu give parallel arguments (equality of moral persons; democracy as institutionalised "rule by virtue") (SEP-MC §§5.2–5.3).

- Li Dazhao (29 Oct 1889 – 28 Apr 1927): chief librarian (from February 1918) and history professor at Peking University; joined *New Youth* board January 1918; first major Chinese intellectual to support the October Revolution (article of July 1918); organised the first Marxist study group in late 1918 (the "Red Chamber"); "Youth and the Villages" (February 1919): "China is a rural nation and most of the laboring class is made up of peasants. If they are not liberated, then our whole nation will not be liberated"; edited the *New Youth* Marxism issue of 1 May 1919 with his "My Marxist Views"; theorised China as a "proletarian nation"; co-founded the CCP (1921) with Chen Duxiu; captured in a raid on the Soviet embassy and hanged by Zhang Zuolin's forces in April 1927. Meisner (1967) stresses his voluntarism. [source: WP-Li_Dazhao]
- Mao Zedong, ***On Practice*** (*Shijian lun*): delivered as a lecture in July 1937 at Yan'an; ***On Contradiction*** (*Maodun lun*): written August 1937, from lectures at the Counter-Japanese (Anti-Japanese Military and Political) University in Yan'an; both later canonised in *Selected Works* vol. 1 (FLP English edition 1965, pp. 311–347 for *On Contradiction*). [source: WP-On_Practice; WP-On_Contradiction]

## I. India: Gandhi (2 Oct 1869 – 30 Jan 1948)
- **Satyagraha** — coinage: the word came from a competition in *Indian Opinion* (South Africa) in 1906 (WP-Satyagraha); Maganlal Gandhi proposed *sadagraha*, which Gandhi altered to *satyagraha* (*satya* "truth" + *āgraha* "firmness/holding firmly"). Other sources date the competition 1907–08; give "1906–08" (see Debates). The published *Apartheid and Its End* chapter says he "first adopted satyagraha … in Johannesburg in 1906" — consistent with the 1906 Transvaal registration (fingerprint) Act campaign. [source: WP-Satyagraha; WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]
- Gandhi: "Truth (*satya*) implies love, and firmness (*agraha*) engenders and therefore serves as a synonym for force. I thus began to call the Indian movement *Satyagraha*, that is to say, the Force which is born of Truth and Love or non-violence, and gave up the use of the phrase 'passive resistance'…" [source: WP-Satyagraha, citing *Satyagraha in South Africa*, Navajivan, pp. 109–10]
- Distinction from "passive resistance": the latter "has admitted of violence as in the case of the suffragettes and has been universally acknowledged to be a weapon of the weak". [source: WP-Satyagraha (Gandhi letter quoted)]
- Thoreau: Gandhi denied deriving civil disobedience from Thoreau; the South African resistance was "well advanced before I got the essay"; he used Thoreau's phrase only to explain the struggle to English readers (letter to P. Kodanda Rao, 10 Sept 1935, CWMG vol. 67 p. 400). [source: WP-Satyagraha]
- Influences: Tolstoy and Thoreau (SEP-PAC); Ruskin's *Unto This Last*, read on a train to Durban in 1904, paraphrased into Gujarati in 1908 as *Sarvodaya* ("the uplift/welfare of all"), which became the name of his social ideal (dignity of labour, equitable distribution, village self-sufficiency); Vinoba Bhave led the later Sarvodaya movement. [source: WP-Sarvodaya; SEP-PAC]
- Ahimsa: Gandhi based nonviolence on self-renunciation (*brahmacharya*, vow taken in 1906) and the "force of love or force of truth that he called satyagraha"; he extended ahimsa to all sentient beings and held that history "has been steadily progressing towards ahimsa". From the *Autobiography*: "Man cannot for a moment live without consciously or unconsciously committing outward himsa (violence)…" [source: SEP-PAC; WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]
- Swaraj: "self-government and not good government"; village-based "oceanic" decentralisation (1946 statement: "Independence begins at the bottom… every village has to be self-sustained"). *Hind Swaraj* itself: cross-ref Age of Empire. [source: WP-Swaraj]
- Means and ends: "Ahimsa and Truth are so intertwined that it is practically impossible to disentangle and separate them… ahimsa is the means; Truth is the end." [source: WP-Satyagraha (citing Gandhi, *Non-violent Resistance*, Dover 2001; original in *From Yeravda Mandir*, not checked)]
- Self-suffering: "the doctrine came to mean vindication of truth, not by infliction of suffering on the opponent, but on oneself" (statement to the Disorders Inquiry Committee, 5 Jan 1920, CWMG vol. 19 p. 206). [source: WP-Satyagraha]
- Campaigns (for dates only; full narrative is in History chapters): Champaran 1917; non-cooperation 1920–22 (called off after Chauri Chaura); Salt March 12 Mar – 6 Apr 1930; Quit India 8 Aug 1942; assassinated by Nathuram Godse 30 Jan 1948. [source: published *Modern South Asia* and *The Interwar Period* chapters; WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]

### ARGUMENT: Gandhi's case for nonviolence (satyagraha)
- Reconstruction (premises from Gandhi's own statements as quoted in WP-Satyagraha and SEP-PAC; the numbering is ours):
  1. The end of action is Truth; no human being possesses the whole truth: "what appears to be truth to the one may appear to be error to the other" (1920 statement).
  2. So we may not inflict suffering on an opponent who may be right; the opponent "must be weaned from error by patience and compassion" (1920).
  3. Means and ends are inseparable ("ahimsa is the means; Truth is the end"); violent means corrupt the end.
  4. Voluntary self-suffering converts the opponent and moves the public, where coercion only compels (SEP-PAC: the Gandhi–King model's power lay in moving large numbers of people).
  5. Therefore the right method of resisting injustice is truth-force: non-cooperation, civil disobedience and self-suffering, never violence.
- **Objection 1 (Orwell, "Reflections on Gandhi", *Partisan Review*, January 1949):** Gandhian methods depend on civil rights and publicity and would not work in a totalitarian state; Orwell cites Gandhi's 1938 view (reported by Louis Fischer, *Gandhi and Stalin*, 1947) that German Jews should have committed collective suicide to rouse the world, and says Gandhi "was merely being honest". Orwell also finds Gandhi's ascetic ideals "inhuman" and holds that his thought presupposes religious faith. [source: WP-Reflections_on_Gandhi (summarising Orwell 1968 ed., pp. 523–31); WS-1, WS-2 (Fischer passage via a blog excerpt — wording only partly verified)]
- **Objection 2 (historical efficacy):** critics argue that Gandhi succeeded only because Britain was exhausted by the world wars and his opponents were relatively sympathetic. [source: SEP-PAC]
- **Objection 3 (the letters to Hitler):** Gandhi wrote to Hitler as "Dear friend" on 23 July 1939 (Wardha; c. 131 words, asking him to prevent war) and 24 December 1940 (c. 1,028 words, calling Nazi acts "monstrous and unbecoming of human dignity"). Sources disagree whether they were intercepted by the British or never sent; neither reached Hitler. Used by critics as evidence of naivety, by admirers as consistency. [source: WS-3 (Time, Biography.com, CS Monitor summaries) — second-hand; one source misdates the first letter to 23 August]
- **Objection 4 (South Africa and Africans):** In a speech of September 1896 Gandhi complained that whites were "degrading the Indian to the level of a raw Kaffir"; Ashwin Desai and Goolam Vahed (*The South African Gandhi: Stretcher-Bearer of Empire*, Stanford UP, 2015) argue he kept Indian grievances separate from Africans', was "on occasion" part of racial stereotyping, and was later reinvented as a saint; in 1906 he led an Indian (later mixed) stretcher-bearer unit during the suppression of the Bambatha (Zulu) rebellion. By 1910 *Indian Opinion* reported discrimination against Africans and Gandhi wrote that Africans "alone are the original inhabitants of the land". Mandela admired him. [source: WP-Mahatma_Gandhi (citing Desai & Vahed, Herman 2008); WS-10; published *Modern South Asia* and *Apartheid and Its End* chapters (consistent)]
- **Objection 5 (consistency):** in 1918 Gandhi recruited Indian combatants for the British army ("the ability to bear arms and to use them"); his secretary Mahadev Desai noted the question of consistency "has been discussed ever since". [source: WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]
- **Objection 6 (Ambedkar):** the 1932 fast was coercion of the weakest party (see §J); in June 1945 Ambedkar dismissed Gandhi's ideas as loved by "blind Hindu devotees" and as a brew of Tolstoy and Ruskin. [source: WP-Mahatma_Gandhi; WS-11]
- **Replies:** (a) Gandhi held that true ahimsa cannot coexist with cowardice — so Indians had to be capable of arms before choosing nonviolence (Christian Bartolf's explanation of the 1918 recruiting) [source: WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]; (b) SEP-PAC: the open question how "absolute" Gandhi's pacifism was; nonviolence as "a total practice of virtue"; the later successes of King, and Mandela/Tutu, as evidence that nonviolence is not tied to one opponent [source: SEP-PAC]; (c) Rosenwald and Pai read Orwell's essay itself as having moved towards Gandhi, judging satyagraha preferable to Western pacifism by 1949 [source: WP-Reflections_on_Gandhi].

## J. Ambedkar (14 Apr 1891 – 6 Dec 1956)
- Mahar (Dalit), born Mhow; Columbia PhD 1927, LSE DSc 1923; influenced at Columbia by John Dewey; called to the bar at Gray's Inn. Mahad satyagraha (1927, right to draw water from a public tank); burned the *Manusmriti* publicly in 1927. [source: WP-B._R._Ambedkar]
- 1935 (Yeola): announced he was born a Hindu but would not die a Hindu (exact wording not verified — paraphrase only). [source: WP-B._R._Ambedkar; WP-Navayana]
- **Communal Award and Poona Pact:** the Communal Award (August 1932, Ramsay MacDonald) gave the "Depressed Classes" separate electorates with 71 seats; Gandhi began a fast unto death in Yerwada jail (from 20 Sept 1932 per WS-11) against separate electorates, which he said would divide Hindu society; the Poona Pact, signed 24 Sept 1932 at Yerwada, replaced them with 148 reserved seats within joint electorates. (WP-Poona_Pact elsewhere says "instead of the originally allotted 80" — an internal inconsistency; the published *Modern South Asia* chapter's 71 matches WP-Communal_Award and WP-B._R._Ambedkar.) Ambedkar signed reluctantly; historians debate compromise or defeat. [source: WP-Poona_Pact; WP-B._R._Ambedkar; published *Modern South Asia*]
- Ambedkar's later verdicts on Gandhi: *Mr. Gandhi and the Emancipation of the Untouchables* (paper for a conference in Quebec, Dec 1942; published Dec 1943, Thacker, Bombay) and *What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables* (June 1945, Thacker): Congress "sucked the juice out of the Poona Pact and threw the rind in the face of the Untouchables". [source: WS-11 (collected works vol. 9 listings)]
- Gandhi's reply to *Annihilation of Caste* ("A Vindication of Caste", *Harijan*, July 1936): defended Ambedkar's right to speak; said Ambedkar chose the wrong texts and interpretations — the *śāstras* he cited could not be accepted as authentic or as the word of God; a religion should be judged "by the very best, not the worst" of its adherents, and Ambedkar's standard "would fail every known living faith". Ambedkar replied in the 2nd ed. (1937, "A Reply to the Mahatma"), arguing (per a WS summary) that ordinary believers do not distinguish genuine texts from interpolations. [source: WP-Annihilation_of_Caste; WS-13] (Ambedkar's own "division of labourers" argument is already reconstructed in Age of Empire.)
- *Who Were the Shudras?* (1946), dedicated to Jyotirao Phule: argues the Shudras were originally an Aryan community of the solar race, ranked within the Kshatriya varṇa, degraded after feuds with Brahmins who refused them *upanayana*; calls "the Aryan race theory" "so absurd that it ought to have been dead long ago". [source: WP-Who_Were_the_Shudras?; WP-B._R._Ambedkar]
- Constitution: Law Minister from August 1947; Chairman of the Drafting Committee from about 29 August 1947 ("two weeks later" after 15 Aug); presented the final draft to Rajendra Prasad 25 Nov 1949; resigned from Nehru's cabinet 27 Sept 1951 after the Hindu Code Bill was blocked. [source: WP-B._R._Ambedkar]
- Conversion: Nagpur, 14 October 1956, taking the Three Refuges and Five Precepts from a monk, then converting about 500,000 followers, prescribing the 22 Vows. Died 6 Dec 1956, three days after finishing *The Buddha and His Dhamma*. [source: WP-B._R._Ambedkar]
- *The Buddha and His Dhamma*: published 1957, posthumously; departs from Theravāda and Mahāyāna by giving *dukkha*, *avijjā* and *nibbāna* social and political meaning; scripture of **Navayāna** ("New Vehicle"), Ambedkar's own name for his Buddhism. Critics (Zelliot and Macy 1980; Anne Blackburn) say its social emphasis excludes or distorts canonical teaching. Companion texts: "Buddha or Karl Marx" and "Buddhism and Communism" (1956); *Revolution and Counter-Revolution in Ancient India*. [source: WP-The_Buddha_and_His_Dhamma; WP-Navayana; WP-B._R._Ambedkar]

### ARGUMENT (short): the Gandhi–Ambedkar dispute over separate electorates (1932)
- Ambedkar: (1) untouchables are a distinct minority whose interests conflict with caste Hindus'; (2) in joint electorates caste-Hindu majorities choose which Dalit candidates win; (3) so only separate electorates guarantee representatives accountable to Dalits. Gandhi: (1) untouchability is a sin within Hinduism that Hindus must remove; (2) separate electorates would divide Hindu society permanently; (3) so reform must come from within, and he fasted. Ambedkar's objection: the fast coerced the weaker party (a "fast unto death" leaves the minority leader responsible for the Mahatma's life). Outcome: reserved seats within joint electorates. [source: WP-Poona_Pact; WP-B._R._Ambedkar; WS-11; the premise-by-premise framing is our reconstruction of the positions those sources report]

## K. Radhakrishnan (5 Sep 1888 – 17 Apr 1975)
- Born Tirutani, a Telugu brahmin, likely Smarta family; schooled at Lutheran and Dutch Reformed mission schools; Madras Christian College (from 1904; MA 1906–08) under A. G. Hogg; his MA thesis on *The Ethics of the Vedanta* answered the missionary charge that Vedānta had no room for ethics. [source: IEP-RAD §1]
- George V Chair of Philosophy, Calcutta, February 1921. *Indian Philosophy*: vol. 1 1923, vol. 2 1927. Upton Lectures (Oxford, 1926) → *The Hindu View of Life* (1927); Hibbert Lectures 1929 → *An Idealist View of Life* (published 1932; IEP calls it his "most sustained, non-commentarial work"). [source: IEP-RAD §1e; date of IVL publication: seed brief, not separately confirmed — IEP gives only "1929 … later published"]
- Knighted 1931; Vice-Chancellor of Andhra University 1931–36; **Spalding Professor of Eastern Religion and Ethics, Oxford, 1936–1952** (first holder; Fellow of All Souls); Vice-Chancellor of Benares Hindu University late 1939 – January 1948; chaired the University Education Commission (report 1949); ambassador to the USSR 1949–52; Vice-President 1952–62; President 1962–67; Bharat Ratna 1954; his birthday is Teachers' Day. [source: IEP-RAD §§1f–g; WP-Sarvepalli_Radhakrishnan; WP-Spalding_Professor…]
- Advaita as universal religion: "The Vedanta is not a religion, but religion itself in its most universal and deepest significance" (HVL 23); religions are interpretations of one intuitive experience; hierarchy of worshippers from "the Absolute" (highest) down to "petty forces and spirits" (HVL 32). On caste: "the system of caste is the outcome of tolerance and trust" (HVL 93). WP: a leading spokesman of "Neo-Vedanta", which reinterpreted Advaita to affirm the reality of the world. [source: IEP-RAD §2c, §2i; WP-Sarvepalli_Radhakrishnan]
- Critics (IEP-RAD §3): (a) circular epistemic authority — experience validated by the Upaniṣads, the Upaniṣads by experience; (b) "East/West" constructions that reproduce orientalist categories; (c) selective evidence — homogenising the West, treating Western mystics as exceptions. Ambedkar's opposite reading of caste is the sharpest contrast (inference, flagged).
- 1929 plagiarism dispute: Jadunath Sinha alleged (*Modern Review*, January 1929) that Radhakrishnan's *Indian Philosophy* II (1927) copied from Sinha's thesis; lawsuits followed; settled in May 1933 on undisclosed terms, all allegations withdrawn; Radhakrishnan's notes had circulated since 1922 according to some scholars. A disputed episode — mention only with both sides. [source: WP-Sarvepalli_Radhakrishnan (Minor 1987; Gopal 1989; Murty & Vohra 1990)]

## L. Aurobindo (15 Aug 1872 – 5 Dec 1950)
- Born Aurobindo Ghose, Calcutta; educated in England from 1879 (St Paul's School; King's College, Cambridge 1890–92); Baroda service; nationalist ("Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, national education", 1906; with Tilak's extremists at Surat 1907; edited *Bande Mataram*); arrested in the Alipore Bomb Case (1908), a year in Alipore jail, acquitted 6 May 1909 (defended by C. R. Das); said he heard Vivekananda's voice in jail; reached French Pondicherry on 4 April 1910 to escape a sedition warrant; withdrew from politics. [source: WP-Sri_Aurobindo]
- *Arya* monthly from 1914 (ceased 1921); *The Life Divine* serialised in *Arya* August 1914 – January 1919; revised as a book: Book One November 1939, Book Two July 1940 (Arya Publishing House, Calcutta). Sri Aurobindo Ashram formed 1926; Mirra Alfassa ("the Mother", b. Paris 1878) ran it after 24 Nov 1926. Epic *Savitri*. [source: WS-9 (Ashram bibliographical note); WP-Sri_Aurobindo]
- Philosophy: against Śaṅkara's world-as-*māyā*, Brahman manifests as the world through *līlā*; evolution from matter to life to mind, because spirit is already "involved" in matter; the goal is not escape but the descent of the divine — the **Supermind** ("Truth-Consciousness"), intermediary between Brahman and the world; man is a "transitional being"; **Integral Yoga**: discovery of the "psychic being" and a threefold transformation (psychic, spiritual, supramental). [source: WP-Sri_Aurobindo; WP-Integral_yoga]
- Nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature 1943 and Peace 1950. Peter Heehs, *The Lives of Sri Aurobindo* (2008), is the standard critical biography. [source: WP-Sri_Aurobindo]

## N. Matilal, Daya Krishna, Mohanty
- Bimal Krishna Matilal (1 June 1935 – 8 June 1991), born Calcutta; taught at the Sanskrit College 1957–62; title *Tarkatīrtha* 1962; Harvard PhD (1962–65) under Daniel Ingalls on the Navya-Nyāya doctrine of negation, also studying with Quine; professor at Toronto; **Spalding Professor at Oxford: 1976–1991 (WP list) or from 1977 (WP Matilal article)** — give "1976/77". Successor to R. C. Zaehner (1952–74), not directly to Radhakrishnan (WP Matilal article's "succeeding Radhakrishnan" is loose). Founding editor of the *Journal of Indian Philosophy*. Padma Bhushan 1990. Died of cancer. [source: WP-Bimal_Krishna_Matilal; WP-Spalding_Professor…]
- Works: *The Navya-Nyāya Doctrine of Negation* (Harvard Oriental Series 46, 1968); *Epistemology, Logic and Grammar in Indian Philosophical Analysis* (1971); *Logic, Language and Reality* (1985); ***Perception: An Essay on Classical Indian Theories of Knowledge*** (Clarendon, 1986); ***The Word and the World: India's Contribution to the Study of Language*** (OUP, 1990); *The Character of Logic in India* (1999, ed. Ganeri); collected essays *Mind, Language and World* and *Ethics and Epics* (2002, ed. Ganeri). [source: WP-Bimal_Krishna_Matilal]
- *Perception* defends a realism close to Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika against Buddhist phenomenalism and idealism (Sautrāntika, Yogācāra), and engages modern sense-data representationalists. [source: WS-12 (publisher/reading-list descriptions)]
- Matilal's analytic style, as used in SEP-EMI (Ganeri): Nyāya has a "potter model" of God (God makes the world from uncreated atoms as a potter from clay), versus a "spider model" (God spins the world from his own essence) and a "magician model" (world as illusion); Matilal (1971: 76) speculated that Nyāya's doctrine of non-overlapping universals (*jāti*, also "caste") may reflect the Brahmanical ideal of a social order without "intermixture of classes". [source: SEP-EMI §§ on God and universals]
- Daya Krishna (1924–2007): Rajasthan University; edited the *Journal of the Indian Council of Philosophical Research* for over three decades; *The Nature of Philosophy* (1955). **"Three Myths about Indian Philosophy" was published in *Diogenes*, July–September 1966, not 1965** (a different essay, "Three Conceptions of Indian Philosophy", appeared in *Philosophy East and West*, January 1965). The three myths: that Indian philosophy is spiritual (aimed at *mokṣa*), that it is authority-bound (to the Vedas), and that it consists of fixed "schools". Reprinted as ch. 1 of *Indian Philosophy: A Counter Perspective* (OUP, 1991; WP lists a 2006 edition). He organised dialogues between Sanskrit pandits and English-writing philosophers. [source: WP-Daya_Krishna; WS-8]
- J. N. Mohanty (1928 – 7 March 2023): born Cuttack; Göttingen PhD 1954; Husserl scholar (*Edmund Husserl's Theory of Meaning*, 1976; *Husserl and Frege*, 1982) and of Indian philosophy (*Classical Indian Philosophy*, 2002; autobiography *Between Two Worlds*, 2002); taught at Calcutta, Oklahoma, Emory, Temple. [source: WP-Jitendra_Nath_Mohanty]

## Debates
- **Was the 1923 debate won by the scientists?** IEP-MCP says Ding's side "prevailed", paving the way for "wholesale Westernization"; IEP-ZJM stresses that Zhang's position shaped a generation and New Confucianism. [source: IEP-MCP §4e; IEP-ZJM]
- **Who counts as a New Confucian?** Narrow (Xiong and his three "followers" plus their students) vs broad (including Liang Shuming, Feng Youlan, Zhang Junmai, Fang Dongmei); SEP-MC calls "disciples of Xiong" misleading; Makeham argues the movement was "retrospectively created". Generational schemes: first (1921–49: Liang, Xiong, Feng), second (1950–79: Mou, Tang, Xu), third (from 1980: Tu, Liu Shu-hsien…). [source: SEP-MC §1; WP-New_Confucianism; Makeham 2003a title per SEP-MC]
- **Mou's Kant:** scholars agree Mou changed Kant's terms; disputed how consciously (IEP-MOU §2a). Whether Mou's critique of Heidegger "misplaces" him (WP-Mou_Tsung-san).
- **Mou's self-negation and democracy:** compatibility or necessity? Practical vs logical necessity; mainland Political Confucianism (Jiang Qing) and meritocracy (Bell, Bai) as rivals. [source: SEP-MC §5]
- **Feng Youlan's legacy:** historian or original philosopher? IEP-FENG: outside China he is seen as a historian; in China as one of the few original philosophers; his Maoist turn remains controversial. [source: IEP-FENG §4]
- **Gandhi on Africans and race:** Desai and Vahed (revisionist) vs accounts of later solidarity and Mandela's admiration. [source: WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]
- **How absolute was Gandhi's pacifism?** (Fiala 2022a; Allen 2022 calls him paradigm of "absolute nonviolence"); 1918 recruiting. [source: SEP-PAC; WP-Mahatma_Gandhi]
- **Orwell's essay:** "grudging" (Menand 2003) vs "well-balanced appreciation" (Williams 2004) vs "one of the sanest, most challenging, and most generous essays" (Rosenwald 2004). [source: WP-Reflections_on_Gandhi]
- **Poona Pact:** compromise or defeat for Dalits? [source: published *Modern South Asia*; WP-Poona_Pact]
- **Navayāna:** faithful recovery of the Buddha's social teaching, or distortion (Zelliot & Macy; Blackburn)? Supporters point to the late dating of caste passages in Buddhist texts. [source: WP-Navayana]
- **Radhakrishnan:** bridge-builder vs orientalist/inclusivist ("Hinduizes all religions"); plagiarism dispute of 1929–33 (settled, allegations withdrawn). [source: IEP-RAD §§2c, 3; WP-Sarvepalli_Radhakrishnan]
- **Daya Krishna vs the "spiritual India" picture** (Radhakrishnan's), and his call to read Indian philosophy through its own categories. [source: WS-8; WP-Daya_Krishna]

- *Modern South Asia* (History): Poona Pact 24 Sept 1932, 148 seats vs Award's 71 — **consistent** with WP-Communal_Award/WP-B._R._Ambedkar (note WP-Poona_Pact's stray "80"). Ambedkar's conversion 1956 at Nagpur with c. half a million — **consistent**. Desai & Vahed quotation — consistent with WP-Mahatma_Gandhi. "Ambedkar charged that Gandhi wrote against caste in English but defended it in Gujarati" — not checked here.
- *Apartheid and Its End*: satyagraha first adopted in Johannesburg in 1906 — consistent with WP-Satyagraha (1906 competition) but note other sources date the coinage 1907–08.
- *History of Peace and Peacemaking*: satyagraha "holding firmly to truth" — consistent.
- *Modern China*: *New Youth* 1915, moved to Beijing 1917; Hu Shih January 1917; "problems and isms" 1919; CCP July 1921 with Chen and Li — **consistent**.
- *Maoism*: *On Contradiction* and *On Practice* written in Yan'an; contradiction universal/"absolute" — **consistent**.
- *American Pragmatism*: Dewey 30 April 1919 – July 1921, Hu as interpreter, "experimentalism" — **consistent** (IEP-FENG adds Feng also studied with Dewey from 1919).
- *Nietzsche*: Lu Xun read Nietzsche in Tokyo from 1906 — **consistent**.
- *Beyond Europe*: "Mou held that Wang understood Confucianism more deeply than Zhu" — consistent but incomplete: Mou ranked the Hu Hong–Liu Zongzhou lineage highest (IEP-MOU §2c). Say so if Mou's lineages are mentioned.
- *Chinese Buddhism and Neo-Confucianism* research notes: the coiner of "Neo-Confucianism" is unresolved there; IEP-MCP here claims Carsun Chang coined it in *The Development of Neo-Confucian Thought* (1957) — **contradicts** the earlier finding of earlier uses (Okakura 1903, Bodde etc.). Do not repeat IEP-MCP's claim.
- *Age of Empire*: Ambedkar Columbia 1927/LSE 1923, Dewey influence; *Annihilation* 1936/1937/1944 — **consistent**. KCB "Svaraj in Ideas": WP now gives October 1931 at Chandannagar; Age of Empire gives "late 1920s or 1931 (disputed)" — not a contradiction.

## NOT VERIFIED — do not use

- Gandhi: "where there is only a choice between cowardice and violence, I would advise violence" ("The Doctrine of the Sword", *Young India*, 11 Aug 1920) — the article and date are confirmed (WS-7), the sentence was not found in any source read. Gandhi's "The means may be likened to a seed, the end to a tree" (*Hind Swaraj*) — not found. Primary site mkgandhi.org was blocked.
- "An eye for an eye makes the whole world blind" and "Be the change you wish to see in the world" — popularly attributed to Gandhi; not checked in any source this session; do not quote as Gandhi's.
- Orwell's sentences on the Jews and "collective suicide" — wording known only from a blog excerpt via WS-2; paraphrase, citing Fischer's *Gandhi and Stalin* (1947) as Orwell's source.
- The text of Gandhi's letters to Hitler beyond "Dear friend" and the short phrase "monstrous and unbecoming of human dignity" (WS-3, second-hand); whether they were intercepted or never sent is disputed.
- A preface by Chen Duxiu to the 1923 debate volume (often stated; WS-4 found no confirmation).
- The exact day of Zhang Junmai's lecture (14 Feb 1923 rests on one WS source) and the month of Ding's reply (April vs June 1923).
- IEP-MCP's claim that Carsun Chang coined "Neo-Confucianism" (see consistency section).
- IEP-MCP's dates where they conflict: Chen Duxiu "1897" (wrong; 1879), Liang Shuming's book "1922" (SEP and WP: 1921), Xiong's *Xin weishi lun* "1944" (that is the vernacular version; the classical version is 1932), Feng's History "first volume 1934, English 1937" (SEP: 1931 and 1934; IEP-FENG: Bodde, Princeton 1952–53).

- The original date of the *Indian Opinion* naming competition (1906 per WP-Satyagraha; 1907 or 1908 elsewhere) — give "1906–08".
- Matilal's Spalding start (1976 vs 1977) — give both.
- Daya Krishna's "Three Myths" in 1965 (as in the brief) — **wrong**: *Diogenes* 1966.
- That Sarojini Naidu was a philosopher — no source.
- Gandhi's 1936 *Harijan* distinction between an ideal *varṇa* and caste — only "judge religion by its best adherents" and "wrong *śāstras*" are sourced (WP-Annihilation_of_Caste).
