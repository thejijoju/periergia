# Philosophy curriculum: plan

**Status: approved by the owner on 18 November 2026 (full plan). The tree is in `src/lib/seed.ts`.**

This is the working plan for Periergia's Philosophy subject. It follows the History curriculum's plan ([../history-curriculum/PLAN.md](../history-curriculum/PLAN.md)): the same audience, the same source-first rule, the same two phases and the same tools.

## 1. Goal and audience

A complete Philosophy curriculum for an **adult general reader**, written as a living textbook: one authored master chapter per node. It has two halves:

- **The history of philosophy**, told as one story from the first arguments in Greece, India and China to the present. This half teaches *who argued what, when, and why it mattered*.
- **The branches of philosophy** (logic, knowledge, reality, mind, ethics, politics, art, science, religion, language). This half teaches *the problems themselves*, and traces each problem across the traditions that worked on it.

The two halves cross-reference each other. The Kant chapter links to the chapters on knowledge, ethics and beauty; the chapter on free will links back to the Stoics, the Buddhists, Spinoza and Hume.

**Genuinely global, as with History.** Indian, Chinese, Islamic, Jewish, Japanese, African and Latin American philosophy are treated as philosophy, with their own chapters *and* inside the thematic chapters. Women philosophers are covered where they actually were, not added at the end. Where scholars disagree, including about how to read a philosopher at all, the chapter names the debate rather than picking a side.

**Philosophy-specific rule: arguments, not just opinions.** Every chapter presents at least one argument step by step (premises, conclusion, the best objection, the reply). Saying "Kant thought X" is not enough; the reader should be able to reconstruct *why*.

## 3. How it will be built

The History method, unchanged:

1. **Phase 1: write everything, source-first.** For each chapter, research notes come first (`docs/philosophy-curriculum/research/`): every date, attribution, quotation and book is verified against a source found during the work. **Memory is never confirmation.** Quotations of philosophers are a particular risk, because so many famous ones are paraphrases or misattributions; each is checked against the text or a reliable translation, and the translation is named. Then the chapter is written from the notes: 1,800–2,500 words, a summary, two checkpoints, a six-question quiz and a verified reading list. Built with the History tools (`docs/history-curriculum/tools/`), checked, double-audited, and merged in batches of two or three.
2. **Phase 2: review every article, one by one**, with two independent audit passes and a claim ledger in `docs/philosophy-curriculum/audits/`.

### Order of work
1. Change the tree in `src/lib/seed.ts` (one PR, once approved).
2. *What Is Philosophy?*
3. A1 → A5, in order, each era's overview last.
4. B → I.
5. Section overviews, then Phase 2.

Progress will be tracked in `docs/philosophy-curriculum/STATUS.md`.

## 4. Questions for the owner
- **Is the size right?** About 115 pieces in total. A shorter first version would be Part A only (about 58 pieces), with the branches later.
- **Branches or history first?** The plan starts with history, because the branch chapters can then link back to it. Starting with Ethics and Logic would put the most-searched topics online sooner.
- **Classics and Religion & Theology** are separate subjects on the site. This plan covers Greek and religious *philosophy* only; Greek literature and theology proper stay in those subjects.
